# Workshop notes: owner + Claude (tab 1)

Running notes from the live planning conversation, 2026-10-16. Owner's words are quoted.

## Topic 1: what the game is about

- Agreed: lamps lit socially. "I think it'll help build people who build common paths together."
- Owner: neglected land going wild is good, "but maybe only for certain biomes".
- Owner: dimming oil exists to stop the whole map being claimed. Land you *build* on may keep an
  oil requirement. Presence-based light, wilderness preserved by presence, "feels good. I want it
  to work well though."

Working model (Claude's proposal, under discussion):
- **Wild land:** turns weekly. Deep biomes (Tangle, Tops) always go wild if nobody's there.
- **Common land:** paths and way-lamps that anyone lights. Lit by presence and passing, it dims
  gently, never lost, and anyone can relight it.
- **Built land:** claimed. Needs oil, which is the brake on sprawl. When oil runs out it goes
  dormant (overgrown, frozen), the buildings are kept, and after a long lapse the land is released
  and the building packs back into the deed.

## Topic 2: Habitica web app vs a standalone game to sell

- Owner: "I'm split between building this as a web-app that habitica players can play and
  building a standalone game I can actually sell."
- Owner: a standalone would be "just a fun game - no real life tie-in at all", sold on Steam
  and/or mobile.
- Consequence: the standalone shares the world, systems and server with the Habitica edition,
  but swaps three edges: where glims come from (gameplay instead of XP), the hero (our own look,
  classes and gear instead of Habitica's), and the art (all our own).
- Claude's proposal: the Habitica edition stays the proving ground. Every new system must pass
  "is this fun with zero Habitica XP?", and Habitica stays at the edges, so the standalone later
  becomes swapping the edges rather than a rewrite.
