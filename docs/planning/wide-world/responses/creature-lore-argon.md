## 2. The whole account: creatures, the Unyoking and the Dark

### 2.1 How everything ties to the Unyoking and the Dark
When **Fallbrine** cut his coercive binding runes into the Deep Measure (**the Unyoking**, §2.2) to force the earth to yield harvest and permanence without anyone's work, he cracked the world's foundation. Two distinct things followed:
1. **The forgetting (the drift and the Turning):** Above the bedrock, the hurting earth struggles to remember itself. Every wick, unheld land **turns**—reaching anew for its trees, streams, and living things. That weekly Turning is not malice; it is the earth *still doing the hard work of trying to remember*.
2. **The Dark (the Draught):** Through **the Crack** came the Dark (§2.5)—aware, cold, and patient, hating rhythm, work, and kept promises.

Every creature a traveller meets in the wide world stands in one of four relations to that wound and that enemy:

### 2.2 The four kinds of life (and non-life)

1. **Ordinary animals (the earth still trying):**
   - *Roster:* Red squirrels and hares (woods and meadows), ducks and kingfishers (lake country), mountain goats (the Tops), glow-moths and blind cave-fish (caves).
   - *What they are and why they exist:* Like oaks, bracken, and wildflowers, ordinary animals belong to the innocent, hurting Wild. Even when the land turns at the wick, they go right back to their small, honest work: goats pick their way up steep cliff switchbacks, ducks gather where fish are thick, kingfishers dart toward clay banks and sunken gravel spits, squirrels cache nuts by turncap logs, and cave-moths flutter toward the nearest mouth's daylight.
   - *In play:* They are harmless (running on the client's shared clock while harmless, zero XP required to enjoy them). Because they are doing their own honest work, watching them teaches a patient walker how to read the land.

2. **Unmoored beasts (animals fevered by the Dark):**
   - *Roster:* Iron-horn beetles and bracken-boars (woods), mire-toads and marsh-crabs (lake country), fevered rams and crag-beetles (the Tops), cave-crickets and rock-back beetles (caves).
   - *What they are and why they exist:* Animals have no pride, greed, or bitterness for the Dark to whisper to (§2.5–2.6). Instead, where the land lies far from tended light and the Dark leans hard upon it, the Dark works on what animals *do* feel: **the disorientation of the Turning, fear, and hunger**. A cold, smoky **fever-crust** gathers on their carapaces and hides, and their eyes cloud with cold violet. They keep their animal nature, but the fever makes lamplight and footsteps sting them like a threat, driving them to charge or lash out at anyone carrying a light.
   - *In play:* When fought down, they can either be slain by force or have their spell broken—shattering the fever-crust off their backs so they recover and flee (or stay behind as peaceful ordinary animals for the rest of the wick).

3. **Things of the Dark (curdled Draught from the Crack):**
   - *Roster:* Gloom-wisps (woods), fen-lanterns (lake country), draught-motes (the Tops), shadow-leeches and silence-tendrils (caves).
   - *What they are and why they exist:* These were never animals and were never alive. They are cold currents of **the Draught** seeping up from the Crack (§2.2, §2.5) that have knotted around a **mockery of care or nature**:
     - A **gloom-wisp** is a cold knot of spite drifting between tree shadows to snipe at lantern-bearers.
     - A **fen-lantern** mimics the warm golden glow of a tended communal lamp from far across the marsh, luring tired walkers off the causeway before turning cold violet when they step within six tiles.
     - A **draught-mote** swirls on high mountain saddles and tries to gust climbers back down a ramp.
     - A **shadow-leech** hugs the dark rim just outside a lantern's circle in a cave, drinking the edge of the light to shrink its radius.
   - *In play:* Because nothing living is trapped inside them, they cannot be redeemed or calmed. When beaten, they **disperse** into cold air, ash, and dim motes.

4. **The Whitequiet's pale harts and white moths (impressions in land that stopped trying):**
   - *What they are and why there are no fights in the Whitequiet:* In the green Wilds, the earth is still working to remember itself. In **the Whitequiet**, Fallbrine's wish for an end to work has come true in the saddest way: the exhausted land has **given up the hard work of remembering** colour, sound, season, and distance, lulling travellers toward a dreamy, sleepy end.
   - Because there is no work, no struggle, and no restless animal fear in the Whitequiet, the Dark has nothing there to twist into a beast-fever, and things of the Dark find no warmth to drink. That is why **the Whitequiet has no fights**.
   - Its only creatures—**silent white moths** and **pale-antlered harts**—are hushed, half-remembered impressions of woodland life, bleached as pale as the feather-grass. The moths spiral gently over buried trinkets that refuse to be forgotten; the pale harts watch from the mist and, if a traveller walks slowly instead of rushing, lead them toward a route stone or the living border.

### 2.3 Why danger grows far from people and light
Care and light anchor the world (§2.6). Around Hearthwick, inside the **Wick Tree's reach** (§2.8), the ground is held still and ordinary animals go about their lives in peace. Out in the turning Wilds, three forces shape how hard the Dark can lean on the land:
1. **The Dark's pressure (the "meta-biome"):** The Crack beneath the earth does not leak evenly. Across wide basins tens of chunks across, the Dark's **pressure** pools heavier in some stretches than others, crossing biome borders like weather beneath the soil (and festering into a monochrome **fret** where it goes completely unchecked).
2. **Distance from people and tended light:** Every tended place—Hearthwick, a held camp, a lit way-lamp, a relit communal lamp—pushes back the cold. Only the **strongest nearby light** counts (never the sum of a cluster, so a row of posts is a road, not a fortress wall). Walk far from tended light and human footsteps, and the land is left alone with the Turning; animals there are much more easily unmoored.
3. **The deep reaches (distance from home):** Across wide bands outward from Hearthwick, the world's old wound lies deeper and longer unwalked. A lit way-lamp in the far Wilds calms its own clearing into a safe harbour, but the surrounding wilds beyond its halo remain deep and sharp.

Once a territory's beasts are calmed or cleared, that stretch of land stays quiet until the weekly **Turning** reshuffles the Wilds (save for a few marked practice sites).

### 2.4 Breaking the spell vs. defeating by force
Just as a party meeting one of **the fallen** (§5.5) can either shatter their binding by force or unwind it to redeem them, every encounter with an **unmoored beast** offers a smaller, everyday moral choice:

- **The stagger moment:** When an unmoored beast is fought down to the edge of defeat (~15% health), its smoky fever-crust cracks and it **staggers**—ceasing its attacks, trembling, its cold violet eyes flickering as the animal underneath tries to surface.
- **Choice 1 — Break the spell (Redeem / Free):**
  - Instead of delivering a killing blow with a weapon, the player answers the fever with **care or craft**: either by **spending a glim / holding up their light** to steady the beast, or by using a **spellcaster's working** (such as a spark of **Fingersnap**, or later Songcraft, Hearthcraft, or Wildcraft workings, which let casters break the fever through their Practice).
  - The fever-crust shatters off the animal's back, dropping **shed carapace, fever-slag, or tangled tuft** on the ground.
  - The beast's eyes clear back to warm brown, it shakes its head, and the fever breaks. It either bounds safely into the brush or **remains in the chunk as a peaceful ordinary animal** for the rest of the wick (a cured goat climbing the crags, a freed boar rooting harmlessly in the bracken).
- **Choice 2 — Defeat by force (Slay):**
  - If the player keeps swinging their weapon through the stagger, they slay the beast outright, harvesting heavier raw trophies (intact horn, meat, heavy shell) at the cost of killing a hurting animal of the Wild.

## 3. Proposed short additions to `canon.md` §2.5 (The Dark)

*Keep §2.5 as written, update the **Unmoored** bullet to cross-reference the expanded §5.4, and add one bullet on **Pressure and things of the Dark**:*

```markdown
- **Pressure and things of the Dark** [later]. The cold from the Crack does not press evenly
  everywhere. Across wide stretches of the Wilds, **pressure** leans heavier where the earth is
  far from tended lamps and human footsteps; a lit lamp calms the ground in its reach (the
  strongest light at a spot, never a stack of them). Where the Draught knots around a mockery of
  light, wind or shadow, it forms **things of the Dark** (5.4): wisps, fen-lanterns and cave
  shadows that were never alive and disperse when beaten.
- **Unmoored** [shipped for the player, later for beasts]. The Dark's first touch. A hero who
  stays too long deep in the Tangle, where the Wick Tree's reach thins (2.8), who lingers in the
  Whitequiet without an anchor (2.4), or who is caught in a Turning, comes **unmoored**: the
  world sways and pales at the edges. It passes with rest on safe ground in town or at home, or
  at once with a remedy (a jar of turncaps; a salve eases it). Wilds lamps are not safe ground.
  Beasts the Dark has fevered are unmoored too (5.4).
```

## 4. Proposed replacement for `canon.md` §5.4

*Replaces `### 5.4 The fallen and the unmoored` (lines 503–516 of `canon.md`) with a complete account of the world's creatures and the fallen, keeping all §12 spellings and `[shipped]` / `[later]` tags:*

```markdown
### 5.4 The fallen, the beasts and the things of the Dark

Everything a traveller meets in the Wilds stands in its own relation to the Turning and the Dark:

- **The fallen** [later]. Fallen people have no single category name. Villagers say someone is
  **lost**; Keepers write that they are **obsessed** or **bound**; children have their own words.
  In the game a fallen person goes by their own name ("Hesper, at the span"), never a label. They
  keep their cunning, craft and workings, now turned to hoarding light and hunting anyone who
  carries a lantern.
- **Ordinary animals** [later]. Squirrels and hares in the woods, ducks and kingfishers by the
  lakes, mountain goats on the Tops, glow-moths and blind fish in the caves. Like the trees and
  streams, they belong to the hurting earth that still tries to remember itself every Turning.
  They are harmless and go about their own small work: a goat climbs a real trail up a crag; a
  kingfisher darts toward clay or a shallow spit; a cave-moth drifts toward daylight. Watching
  them helps a traveller read the land.
- **Unmoored beasts** [shipped as beetles; overhaul later]. Beetles, boars, mire-toads, marsh-crabs,
  rams and cave-crickets. Animals have no pride or greed for the Dark to whisper to, so where the
  land lies far from tended light and the Dark's pressure leans in, it works on their fear, hunger
  and confusion at the Turning. A smoky **fever-crust** gathers on them and turns them against
  lamplight. When fought down to the point of breaking, a beast staggers and a player may choose
  how to end the fight:
  - **Break the spell.** Answer the fever with a **glim**, a raised light or a **working** (such
    as Fingersnap or a Practice's calming craft). The fever-crust shatters off as shed material,
    the animal's eyes clear, and it flees into the brush—or stays in that place as an ordinary
    animal for the rest of the wick.
  - **Defeat by force.** Strike through the stagger with a weapon to slay the beast outright for
    its intact parts and trophies.
- **Things of the Dark** [shipped as wisps; variants later]. Wisps in the woods, **fen-lanterns**
  in the marshes (which mimic the warm gold of a communal lamp from afar before turning cold
  violet up close), **draught-motes** on windy passes, and **shadow-leeches** along the rim of a
  lantern's light in caves. They were never animals and were never alive: they are knots of cold
  from the Crack formed around a mockery of light, wind or shadow. Nothing living is trapped
  inside them; when beaten, they disperse into cold air and ash.
- **Creatures of the Whitequiet** [later]. In the Whitequiet the exhausted earth has stopped
  trying to remember itself, so there is no struggle for the Dark to fever and no warmth for
  things of the Dark to drink. The Whitequiet has no fights. Its **white moths** and **pale
  harts** are hushed impressions of woodland life: moths spiral above buried trinkets, and a
  pale hart will lead a slow, patient walker toward a route stone or the living border.
- **Holdouts** [later]. A few survivors of the Company still hold out alone in remote corners of
  the Wilds, wary, waiting to see whether the new Hearthwick keeps its word.
```
