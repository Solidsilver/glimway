# Wide world brainstorm: biomes, mountains, lakes, caves and landmarks

*Ideas for 0.14 and beyond. Grounded in `canon.md`, the synthesis decisions, and the screen-space
height and layer −1 cave model from `height-spike-notes.md`.*

---

## 1. Biomes beyond the first four

The first four biomes are **Hearthwick woods and meadow** (made richer with groves, glades, faint
paths and bramble thickets), **lake country**, **uplands with mountains (the Tops)**, and **the
Whitequiet**. Below are twelve more, ordered from gentle and near-home to strange and far-flung.
Every biome is driven by the lasting fields (wetness, upland/height, warmth, and an amber/mineral
vein field) while **pressure** (how hard the Dark leans on the land) crosses them independently.

### 1.1 The Brackenwood (Iron-Oak & Copper Fern)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Near-home old-growth timberland where the company once logged
  iron-oak. Best 1–3 biome regions out from Hearthwick or flanking the old Lantern Road.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Wide, dark-barked iron-oak trunks (2×2 tile footprints with roots
  clutching stones) spaced far enough apart that you walk under a high, dappled canopy rather than
  squeezing through scrub. The floor is a warm rust-and-amber carpet of waist-high bracken fronds
  that part by a pixel or two when you step through. Sunbeams fall as soft diagonal amber patches.
- **What you do:** Fell marked windfall iron-oak (never unmarked live trees—that's "stealing
  shade"), forage turncaps tucked against warm roots, clear unmoored stag-beetles from old skidding
  tracks, and build cosy timber camps in natural sawing-glades.
- **What lives there:** Ordinary red squirrels and nut-jays (which hop toward hidden resin caches);
  unmoored iron-horn beetles whose carapaces clack like cast iron; low-pressure wisps only where a
  charter-mark was defaced.
- **Lore & borders:** The Oak Hall's old territory. Meets **meadows** through hazel coppices and
  old stacked-stone boundary walls; meets **the Tops** as the oaks stunt into wind-bent scrub along
  level-1 cliff toes.

### 1.2 The Oaker Hills (Charcoal Downs & Coppiced Birch)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Rolling level-0 to level-1 chalk-and-clay downs dotted with
  silver birch rings and the turf-covered mounds of old charcoal clamps. Feels right near to
  mid-range (west toward Tarrow-on-the-Reed).
- **Top-down look & feel:** Pale green-gold fescue grass with white chalky sheep-tracks ribbing the
  contours. Silver-white birch trunks read instantly against dark soot-rings where burners once
  worked. Cliff faces here are only 1 level high—pale chalk scarps with green turf lips.
- **What you do:** Gather birch bark, smudge-charcoal (for filtering oils and firing kilns) and
  flint nodules; follow sheep-runs that naturally lead to easy ramps; build hillside homesteads
  with clean views down into valleys.
- **What lives there:** Downland hares that bolt along contour lines toward safe passes; unmoored
  rams that charge down ramps if fevered; soot-motes (things of the Dark that mimic drifting ash
  around cold clamps).
- **Lore & borders:** Canon's charcoal-burner country over the ridge toward Tarrow. Bleeds into
  **lake country** through willow-lined chalk streams, and steps up into **the Tops** via terraced
  sheep-walks.

### 1.3 The Sapping Grounds (Amberwash & Pine Barrens)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Sandy, resin-scented pine flats and pale gravel washes where
  ancient amber-bearing trees weep gold into the soil. Mid-distance, warm and dry.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Rust-orange pine needles over cream-coloured gravel scars. Tall,
  straight red pines have high crowns (drawn with a translucent canopy fade when you walk beneath)
  so the ground plane stays clean and readable. In shallow washes, glints of raw amber sparkle as
  2-frame pixel highlights in the gravel.
- **What you do:** Pan gravel bars for candle-grade and hearth-grade amber drops, tap
  resin-blisters, mend old company sluice-boxes, and defend resin camps from amber-drawn pests.
- **What lives there:** Amber-finches and pine-martens; unmoored resin-hounds and sap-borer beetles
  sticky with golden pitch; things of the Dark that look like black, light-drinking tar-slimes in
  high-pressure pockets.
- **Lore & borders:** The reason Hearthwick was settled (~155 years ago). Meets **Brackenwood**
  along sandy fire-breaks and **lake country** where gravel washes fan out into yellow-sand deltas.

### 1.4 The Merrow Saltings (Tidal Fen & Sea-Lavender Marsh)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Far south-west coastal fenland of braided brackish channels,
  salt-pans, and purple sea-lavender beds.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Flat, wide-sky country. Ground tiles shift from silver-grey slick mud
  to tussocky cord-grass and carpets of violet sea-lavender. Narrow tidal creeks wind in S-curves
  with crisp 1-pixel white salt rims. Raised wooden duckboards and gravel causeways give clear dry
  routes across the wet.
- **What you do:** Harvest salt-crust, marsh-samphire, reed-thatch and bog-iron; fish brackish
  pools for silver-eel; walk raised drift-paths that shift slightly every Turning except where
  stone tide-marks hold them.
- **What lives there:** Long-legged stilts and tide-foxes (ordinary foxes that trot along the
  safest causeways before vanishing into reedbeds); unmoored marsh-crabs the size of wheelbarrows;
  brine-wisps that drift over deep black mud-holes.
- **Lore & borders:** Elara Quill's homeland, where people ride the drift with drift-tables instead
  of fighting it. Meets **lake country** through reed-choked estuaries and **meadows** via
  salt-meadows grazed by wild ponies.

### 1.5 Sallow Fen (Alder Carr & Oil-Reed Bottoms)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Low, wet woodland of multi-stemmed black alders, yellow flag-iris
  and oil-seed beds in the north-east approaches toward Sallow Ford.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Dark, glossy water pooled between mossy root-islands (hummocks). Every
  tree grows on a slightly dry 3×3 moss pad surrounded by shallow walk-slowing water or black silt.
  Golden-headed oil-reeds and yellow irises provide bright colour contrast from above so walkable
  vs unwalkable water is unmistakable.
- **What you do:** Harvest oil-reeds and tallow-berries, lay log-corduroy roads across the mire (a
  great communal project), and search for half-sunken cargo from the Great Carting.
- **What lives there:** Kingfishers (their blue flash points toward deep fishing holes) and
  pond-skaters; unmoored mire-toads whose croaks push walkers back; fen-lanterns (things of the
  Dark that imitate a warm communal lamp colour until you get within 6 tiles, when their light
  turns cold violet).
- **Lore & borders:** The wet belt before Sallow Ford. Transitions out of **lake country** where a
  lake's outflow loses its banks, and borders **the Whitequiet** along pale, mist-hung willow
  fringes.

### 1.6 Heather Moor & Peat Hag (High Blanket Bog)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Windy level-1 tablelands between woods and high peaks—vast
  purple-and-russet heather expanses broken by dark peat hags (1-level-deep eroded gullies).
- **Top-down look & feel:** Sweeping violet heather and tawny deer-grass with almost no trees to
  block sightlines, making the horizon feel huge. Peat hags read as dark chocolate-brown
  mini-cliffs (1 row of face art instead of 3) winding like labyrinth trenches through the purple
  moor, with cotton-grass tufts waving white pixel heads along wet edges.
- **What you do:** Cut peat turves for long-burning hearth fuel, gather heather-honey and
  bog-myrtle, leap or plank across peat trenches, and hunt for ancient bronze and stone tools
  preserved in the peat.
- **What lives there:** Red grouse that flush with a clatter when stepped near, and highland hares;
  unmoored moor-hounds; under high pressure, "the Draught" blows visibly across the heather as grey
  wind-streaks.
- **Lore & borders:** Sits naturally on level-1 shoulders below **the Tops**, and drains down into
  **lake country** via amber-tinted peat burns.

### 1.7 Ash-Fell & Cinder Scree (Cold Volcanic Uplands)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Far-out, stark upland basins of black basalt columns, silver-grey
  pumice scree, and bright lime-green lichen.
- **Top-down look & feel:** High-contrast and geometric. Instead of soft rounded cliffs, level
  faces here are hexagonal basalt steps (crisp vertical pixel ribs). The ground is dark slate-grey
  ash punctuated by neon-green lichen cushions and steaming fumarole vents that puff white pixel
  rings.
- **What you do:** Mine obsidian glass, pumice (for grinding lenses and lamp-panes) and
  sulphur-salts; warm up beside hot vents (which act as weak natural safe-rest spots against being
  unmoored); climb stepped basalt columns.
- **What lives there:** Cinder-newts that bask on warm stones; unmoored basalt-back beetles that
  curl into rolling boulders down ramps; ash-wraiths of the Dark in cold craters.
- **Lore & borders:** Places where the Deep Measure's stone was scorched when the Unyoking's runes
  cracked below. Meets **the Tops** along sharp dykes of black rock.

### 1.8 The Gilded Orchard (Wilding Cider-Woods)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Sheltered, sun-warmed valleys mid-way out where pre-Unyoking or
  early Settling fruit groves went wild—gnarled crab-apple, medlar, quince and walnut trees.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Round, dome-crowned trees in blossom (pink-white in Mudrise/Carting) or
  heavy with red-and-gold fruit (Amberfall), set in long waist-high meadow grass starred with
  dandelions and tumbled dry-stone walls.
- **What you do:** Shake trees for fruit, collect wild beeswax (for candle-making and woodworking
  polish), prune overgrown grafting-stocks, and claim old walled orchard plots for Wilds hearths.
- **What lives there:** Hedgehogs, badgers and honey-bees; unmoored orchard-boars rooting furiously
  in the windfalls; wasp-swarms fevered by the Dark.
- **Lore & borders:** Remnants of smallholdings from the Settling and the Weir Years that turned
  back to the Wilds when families moved inward. Blends softly into **meadows** and **Brackenwood**.

### 1.9 Karst Pavement & Sinkhole Down (Limestone Country)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Mid-to-far upland plateaus where the soil has washed away,
  leaving pale grey limestone slabs cross-hatched by deep fissures (grykes) full of ferns.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Like a giant weathered flagstone floor from above—bone-white and
  dove-grey rock blocks separated by dark 2-pixel cracks sprouting hart's-tongue fern and rowan
  saplings. Natural sinkholes (2×2 or 3×3 dark pits) drop straight into layer −1 caves.
- **What you do:** Quarry dressed limestone for tier-2 house building, gather lime-loving herbs in
  the fissures, and descend into the richest cave networks in the game.
- **What lives there:** Rock-doves and slow-worms; unmoored cave-crickets that leap across
  fissures; shadow-tendrils reaching up from high-pressure sinkholes.
- **Lore & borders:** Here the Deep Measure's bedrock lies bare to the sky. Borders **the Tops** as
  lower limestone benches and meets **lake country** in disappearing rivers (swallow-holes).

### 1.10 A Fret (Drained Valley / Blight-Hollow)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Canon §2.5's **fret**—not a permanent climate biome, but a rare,
  high-pressure biome/meta-biome intersection far from town where the Dark has festered unchecked
  in a valley or marsh.
- **Top-down look & feel:** All saturation is drained out: trees stand as silver-charcoal
  skeletons, grass is frost-grey even in summer, and water is flat ink-black without reflections.
  No bird sprites, no wind animation in the foliage—eerie visual stillness. When a player carries a
  lantern or lights a way-lamp, a warm full-colour circle blooms around the light, literally
  repainting the grass and leaves inside its radius.
- **What you do:** High-stakes expeditions with friends: carry oil, build a chain of way-lamps or
  perform Songcraft/Hearthcraft workings to push the fret back, harvest rare shadow-tempered
  iron-oak and night-quartz, and rescue trapped animals.
- **What lives there:** No ordinary animals until lit; heavily fevered unmoored beasts (wolves,
  stags) and dense concentrations of things of the Dark (gloam-stalkers, silence-wells).
- **Lore & borders:** Pure canon (§2.5). Its border is a sharp "colour line" where normal grass
  pales over 4–5 tiles into monochrome ash-grey.

### 1.11 The Glass-Sand & Singing Dunes (Wind-Scoured Basin)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Far-flung rain-shadow basins behind high mountain ranges: pale
  quartz sand, wind-carved sandstone hoodoos, and dry riverbeds (wadis) that flash with water only
  in Mudrise.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Rippled cream-and-rose sand tiles with crisp dark cast shadows from red
  sandstone spires (1–2 levels tall, narrow footprints). Walkers leave temporary pixel footprints
  behind them.
- **What you do:** Gather pure silica sand for clear lamp-glass and lenses, dig dry streambeds for
  sun-cured storm-grade amber, and trace ancient pre-Compact caravan cairns.
- **What lives there:** Sand-martins and horned lizards; unmoored glass-scorpions; mirage-wisps
  that shimmer in heat-haze.
- **Lore & borders:** Where the wind has ground old rune-scarred stone into glass-sand. Meets
  **Karst** or **Ash-Fell** through rocky canyon mouths.

### 1.12 The Deep-Root Groves (Wick-Scion Woods)
- **What it is & where it sits:** Extremely rare, small (4–8 chunk) pockets very far out where a
  seedling or root-runner of the Wick Tree's ancient kin still touches the Deep Measure.
- **Top-down look & feel:** Immediately arresting: the canopy leaves are pale gold and bronze all
  year, the undergrowth glows with faint warm bioluminescent moss dots, and a massive silver-barked
  scion-tree anchors the centre.
- **What you do:** A natural sanctuary far from home. Rest without using glims to clear being
  unmoored, tap a modest trickle of wild sap, and build a chartered Wilds hearth around the grove.
- **What lives there:** White harts, golden moths, and friendly badgers; no things of the Dark can
  enter the grove's core chunk, though they prowl the perimeter just outside its golden leaf-litter
  line.
- **Lore & borders:** Connects the Wick Tree (§2.8, §3.2) to the wider world without cheapening
  Hearthwick—these scions are small, sleepy cousins that hold only a couple of chunks still.

## 2. Mountains

With the height lab's screen-space rule (surface levels 0–3, each cliff drop taking 3 south-facing
face rows, ramps and single-tile-wide switchbacks, lower levels fading toward fog or sky),
mountains become tactile puzzles rather than painted walls.

### 2.1 Kinds of ranges and peaks
- **Stepped Tors & Table Hills (Levels 1–2):** Flat-topped moorland tables ringed by dark gritstone
  crags, with grassy benches halfway up. Easy to build a watch-camp on; common near the Oaker Hills
  and the Tops.
- **Hogback Ridges (Levels 1–3):** Long, narrow spines stretching 15–30 chunks north-west to
  south-east. They act as real geography barriers: you can't just walk straight west without
  finding a pass, a switchback trail, or a tunnel underneath.
- **Cirque & Tarn Peaks (Levels 0–3):** Horseshoe-shaped level-3 ridges cupping a cold level-1 or
  level-2 mountain tarn (lake) in their lap, spilling out over a multi-level waterfall to the
  south.
- **The Broken Teeth (Jagged Pinnacles, Level 3):** Small, steep level-3 islands atop level-2
  shoulders, reachable only by narrow 1-tile stone staircases or goat-tracks where two players have
  to step aside at passing-bays.

### 2.2 Trails, passes, saddles and ledges
- **Switchback Goat-Tracks:** As proven in the height lab, three 1-tile legs lying side by side
  across a cliff face, joined only at their hairpin turns (where a flat 2×2 landing lets you pause,
  fight a single beetle, or pass a friend).
- **Wind-Gaps & Saddles (Level 1 between Level 3s):** Where a hogback ridge dips down to level 1
  for 4–6 tiles between two towering level-3 peaks. The generator naturally routes old roads,
  desire paths and wind through these saddles. Often marked by a weathered route stone or a
  half-ruined toll-booth from the Carters' Compact.
- **Sheep-Walk Ledges:** Narrow 1- or 2-tile-wide level-1 strips clinging to the south face of a
  level-3 cliff. Walking along one with the valley faded in fog below and sheer rock behind you
  feels thrillingly high in 3/4 view.
- **Chimney Climbs & Screes:** A steep 1-tile slot cut into a cliff face (drawn as dark notched
  handholds) where your walker moves at half-speed with a climbing bob, paired with a loose scree
  chute nearby that only allows one-way sliding *down* from level 2 to level 1.

### 2.3 How a range is crossed
Crossing a major mountain range (20+ chunks long) should feel like a mini-expedition with three
distinct choices:
1. **The High Pass (Surface Level 1–2 Saddle):** Follow an old contour trail up ramps and
   switchbacks to a windy saddle between peaks. Exposed to upland beasts and cold wind, but keeps
   you in daylight with grand views and a route stone to relight at the crest.
2. **The Under-Road (Layer −1 Tunnel):** Enter a cave mouth at the range's southern foot and thread
   through dark chambers and subterranean ramps to emerge on the northern slope. Sheltered from the
   surface climb, but requires lantern oil, courage in the dark, and navigating cave life.
3. **The River Gorge (Level 0 Notch):** Rare places where a major river has sawed a narrow canyon
   straight through a range. Flat walking, but requires wading fords, mending broken ledges or
   rebuilding a washed-out footbridge.

### 2.4 Waterfalls, summits and things only seen from up high
- **Multi-Level Waterfalls:** Because south-facing cliffs show 3 face rows per level, a stream
  spilling south off level 2 to level 0 paints a gorgeous 6-tile-tall white-and-cyan foam cascade
  down the rock face, ending in a mist-ringed plunge pool at the bottom (and sometimes hiding a
  cave mouth right behind the curtain of water).
- **What's at a summit (Level 3):**
  - **Fire-Beacons & Survey Cairns:** Old stone braziers or trig-pillars built by the Sallow Ford
    Institute of Cartography. Lighting a summit beacon with oil reveals a huge circle of lasting
    terrain contours on your world map.
  - **Sky-Ponds & Cloud-Berry Mats:** Tiny rainwater pools that never dry, bordered by golden
    cloud-berries and storm-lichen used in Waycraft workings.
  - **Eagle Eyries & Meteoric Iron:** Nests of giant crag-eagles containing shiny lost trinkets,
    and rust-red meteorites embedded in the bare peak rock.
- **Things you can only see from up high:**
  - When standing on level 2 or 3 (as the camera eases out or snaps to a wider whole-pixel zoom and
    lower levels fade into pale blue-grey fog), **distant glints** render over the fog: the golden
    pinpoint of a friend's lit way-lamp 20 chunks away, a smoke plume from an undiscovered holdout
    camp, or the pale ribbon of a far river.

## 3. Lakes and water

Lakes are lasting features of the Deep Measure with permanent IDs, walkable shores, reeds and clay.

### 3.1 Kinds of lakes, rivers and marshes
- **Kettle Meres:** Round, deep, dark-blue woodland lakes (2–4 chunks across) with steep mossy
  banks, floating white water-lilies, and submerged iron-oak logs.
- **Ribbon Lakes & Finger Lochs:** Long, narrow lakes filling glacial troughs between level-2
  ridges (6–15 chunks long), with pebble beaches at the shallow ends and cliff faces dropping
  straight into deep water along the sides.
- **High Tarns:** Cold, crystal-clear turquoise pools perched on level-1 or level-2 mountain
  shelves.
- **Braided Gravel Rivers (like the upper Wend & the Reed):** Wide rivers split by pale shingle
  islands, log-jams, and shallow riffles where water sparkles over stones.
- **Spring-Heads & Boils:** Places at the foot of a limestone cliff where an underground river
  wells up from layer −1 in a bubbling turquoise circle, warm enough to keep ice off in Quiet-wick.

### 3.2 Shores, islands, fords and stepping stones
- **Readable Shore Zones:** In top-down pixel art, a good lake edge has three distinct rings:
  1. **Dry bank:** willow trees, alder clumps and dry-grass tufts;
  2. **Wet clay & shingle shore:** footprints darken the ground for a few seconds and blue-grey
     clay nodes spawn;
  3. **Shallow wadeable fringe (1–2 tiles wide):** the walker's feet submerge with a tiny white
     ripple ring, and tall green reed-beds grow before the dark, impassable deep water begins.
- **Crannogs & Hermit Isles:** Small islands out in the middle of a lasting lake—some reachable
  only by wading a sunken gravel spit that snakes just beneath the surface (readable by a paler
  teal water colour and tiny surface ripples), others waiting for a player-built wooden footbridge
  or a tethered chain-punt (like a miniature Sennick's Ferry).
- **Sallow-Style Fords & Stepping Stones:** Natural river crossings where the river widens and
  shallows over flat bedrock slabs or a line of mossy boulder stepping-stones, often guarded by a
  dark lamp stone on either bank.

### 3.3 What's fun to do by water in a top-down game
- **Lazy-Simulated Wild Fishing:** Casting from reed-gaps, wooden jetties or stepping-stones into
  dark fish-shadows; reading the water (rings on the surface show whether a lake is well-stocked or
  nearly fished out; different species bite in deep Kettle Meres vs fast gravel riffles).
- **Clay Digging & Reed Cutting:** Gathering wet blue-grey river clay for Aldo's kiln (pottery,
  roof tiles, brick hearths) and cutting reed bundles for thatch, baskets and lamp-wicks.
- **Skimming Stones & Floating Toy Hulls:** A zero-XP cosy toy: pick up flat shore-slates and skim
  them across the water (counting the pixel bounces with friends), or launch tiny candle-lit bark
  boats down the current as villagers do at **The Breaking** (canon §9).
- **Drift-Flotsam Beachcombing:** After every weekly Turning, the lee shore of a big lake or river
  bend collects a few washed-up items: driftwood, lost cork floats, amber pebbles, or a carved
  wooden creature.

## 4. Caves and below

Caves live on layer −1 at the exact same `(x, y)` coordinates as the surface above them, joined by
seamless portal arches (or sinkholes) where daylight fades over 6 tiles into radial darkness around
your light.

### 4.1 Cave kinds
- **Root-Cellars & Badger-Holts (1–2 chunks):** Shallow, dry earthen caves tucked under ancient
  iron-oaks or level-1 banks. Tree roots hang from the ceiling (drawn as warm brown fringes along
  the wall tops), and turncaps grow in clusters.
- **Winding Limestone Descents & Swallow-Holes (3–5 chunks):** Entered where a surface stream
  plunges into a dark arch. Inside, a narrow underground brook runs beside a slick limestone path
  through keyhole passages into a vaulted dripstone hall of stalagmites and black mirror-pools.
- **Under-Mountain Passes (4–8 chunks):** Caves that enter at the south foot of a level-3 mountain
  range and exit on the north side—or climb internally via subterranean stone ramps to emerge from
  a mouth high on a level-2 shoulder (just like the height lab's east tunnel). Sometimes faster
  than climbing over the snow-line, if you dare the dark.
- **Crystal & Glow-Worm Grottoes:** Chambers where veins of pale blue moon-spar, amber-quartz or
  canopy constellations of bioluminescent glow-worms punch natural soft light holes out of the cave
  dark overlay.
- **Flooded Echo-Vaults:** Quiet underground lakes where you walk along narrow stone causeways and
  natural rock bridges above black water that reflects your lantern light.
- **Old Company Delvings & Short-Wayer Workings:** Square-cut mine adits shored up with iron-oak
  timbers, rusted ore-cart rails, and—in the deepest, rarest chambers—cracked geometric stone
  floors bearing fragments of Fallbrine's old binding runes from the Unyoking.

### 4.2 How light works as a feature below ground
- **Light is terrain:** In layer −1, darkness isn't just an aesthetic tint; it's the curtain. Your
  hero's personal light shows a modest close circle (enough to walk safely, never pitch-blind).
  Carrying a lit torch or lantern widens it; setting a **communal lamp** or **way-lamp** down
  carves a permanent, warm golden clearing in the dark that stays lit for everyone who walks
  through.
- **Light-reactive ecology & puzzles:**
  - **Sun-wedges at mouths:** Over the 6 portal entry tiles, pale daylight angles in, growing moss
    and cave-ferns that point the way back out even if you get turned around.
  - **Shining crystals (Mirror-Spar):** Hitting a resonant crystal with **Fingersnap** (or holding
    a lantern beside it) causes it to flare for 30 seconds, lighting up a whole chamber to reveal
    hidden ledges, ore veins or a sleeping beast across a chasm.
  - **Shy & light-drawn life:** Blind white cave-fish and glow-beetles gather gently toward a
    resting lantern, while shadow-creatures flinch back to the dark rim of a way-lamp's radius.

### 4.3 What you find down there & underground bases
- **Finds:** Deep-measure slate, bog-iron and silver-lead veins, pure cave-pearls, luminous fungi,
  ancient tools left by pre-Unyoking delvers, and lost caches from Company prospectors.
- **Underground Hearths (Places worth building a base):**
  - **Skylight Caverns (Cenotes):** A large layer −1 hall where the ceiling has collapsed in the
    middle, letting a 4×4 shaft of surface sunlight, rain and a cascading vine-waterfall drop into
    a lush underground garden patch surrounded by dry, sheltered cave rock. You get the safety and
    cosy enclosure of a cave with natural light at its heart—an unforgettable spot to co-sign a
    Wilds hearth charter.
  - **Warm Spring Grottos:** Steam-heated caverns with a glowing mineral pool, flat dry stone
    terraces ready for furniture and workbenches, and natural acoustic resonance for Songcraft.

## 5. Landmarks and small wonders

Small (1×1 to 6×6 tile) procedural set-pieces the generator can scatter so a ten-minute walk always
has a story: "turn west at the lightning oak, then follow the brook to the sunken cart."

1. **Dark Lamp Stones & Leaning Route Stones (Canon §3.5):** Weathered stone pillars along
   forgotten spurs of the road. Some bear Wenna's closure mark (*two weaves and a break*); others
   have half-mossy Keeper cuts you can copy into your notebook or relight with a lamp wick to
   create a free communal rest-light.
2. **Sunken Compact Carts:** A broken wooden wagon axle and spoked wheel half-swallowed by bracken
   or fen mud, from the Weir Years. Searching it yields stamped road-nails, old iron-oak planks, or
   a tarnished wheel-tax token.
3. **Witness Oaks (Charter Trees):** A massive 3×3 ancient iron-oak bearing the Oak Hall's carved
   mark (*a wheel and a wave*) in its bark. The 5-tile clearing around its roots is naturally calm
   (slightly reduced Dark pressure) and never spawns brambles.
4. **Cowl-Roofs & Ruined Bothies:** A tumbledown stone shepherd's hut or charcoal-burner's shelter
   with its roof collapsed, except for the stone chimney topped by a rusted iron **cowl** that
   still points stubbornly toward the nearest held light—and a ring of grey **turncaps** growing
   around the hearthstone inside.
5. **Measure-Cracks (Hairline Scarps):** A narrow, ruler-straight 1-tile cleft in bare bedrock
   where a geometric binding rune from the Unyoking cracked open. Faint cold draught rises from it
   (or, if soothed with a glim and a song, it hums warmly and yields a shard of rune-stone).
6. **Hot Springs & Steaming Basins:** Tiered travertine pools of pale milky-blue water tucked
   against a cliff foot. Sitting on the warm stone rim for ten seconds clears the **unmoored** sway
   and dries wet clothes.
7. **Lookout Cairns & Bench-Overlooks:** Placed automatically on level-2 or level-3 cliff lips
   facing south over a valley or lake. Stepping up to the cairn eases the camera out an extra notch
   and shows the names of any named way-lamps visible in the valley below.
8. **Fairy Rings & Whispering Stones:** A neat circle of pale mushrooms or knee-high standing
   stones in a meadow glade. At the Turning, the ring stays while the trees around it shuffle;
   standing in the centre lets you hear a faint harmonic chord pointing toward the nearest
   unsettled Echo.
9. **Abandoned Mill-Races & Weir Steps:** Stone-lined water channels and mossy sluice-gates beside
   a river from the Weir Years, offering free dressed stone, a bridge crossing, and a deep
   mill-pool rich with trout.
10. **Lightning-Split Scions:** A hollow, blackened giant tree trunk struck by storm-lightning, its
    interior glowing with golden amber-resin tears that hardened in the heat (**storm-grade
    amber**).

## 6. The Whitequiet as a place

Per the owner's decision, **the Whitequiet is now its own biome**—not a world-closing wall, and
with **no fights**. Its danger is coming **unmoored** and getting lost; its gift is memory
(**trinkets** and **Echoes**, canon §2.4 & §2.7).

### 6.1 How it looks and feels to walk through
- **Visual palette:** A hushed, high-key palette of silver-birch white, frost-ivory, pale dove-grey
  and soft winter-sky blue—not snow, but *bleached by forgetting*, as if painted in watercolour
  where the pigment thinned out.
- **Top-down readability:** Grass tufts are white-silver feather-grass; trees are slender, leafless
  white aspens and silver willows that cast long, pale lavender shadows. The ground has a soft,
  chalky paper texture. Crucially, **your own hero and any lit lantern keep their full, rich warm
  colours**, so you and your friends look like living embers walking across a page of vellum.
- **Sound & motion:** Wind and footstep sounds drop to a whisper. Leaves don't rustle unless you
  brush past them. There are no aggressive red enemy telegraphs anywhere—the relief of knowing
  nothing will jump out and bite you makes the quiet sink in.

### 6.2 How its danger works (Unmoored & Lost)
- **The Unmooring Drift:** Without the Wick Tree's reach, staying in the Whitequiet slowly builds
  the **unmoored** state (canon §2.5): the screen edges pale, a soft compass-sway sets in, and your
  map window gently blurs outside your immediate sight radius (unless you stand near a lit way-lamp
  or eat a turncap remedy).
- **Subtle Veil-Mist:** When you're unmoored in the Whitequiet, distant landmarks beyond 12 tiles
  fade into white mist, and desire paths don't scuff into the pale grass from ordinary
  footsteps—only laid stone paths or named lamp-chains hold a line through it. You really have to
  navigate by stone ("walk by stone, not by path", canon §9), following compass-leaning turncaps,
  old route stones, or a friend's lantern.

### 6.3 What you find & why you'd go there
- **Trinkets surfacing in the grass:** Because the Whitequiet keeps what was made with care and
  gives it back (§2.7), small warm-coloured objects glint unmistakably against the white ground: a
  dropped copper kettle, a bundle of seasoned iron-oak staves, a carter's brass lantern-hook, or
  story keepsakes.
- **Echoes of the Road & the Old World:** Phantom, warm-sepia vignettes waiting in the pale
  glades—not only the Six, but small everyday moments from the Settling and the Weir Years (a pair
  of surveyors arguing over a chain, a child floating a wooden boat, a lamplighter humming a
  Songcraft cadence). Paying a few glims to light the **owed lamp** lets the moment finish kindly,
  rewarding you with lost library recipes, Songcraft verses, lamp ash, or rare materials.
- **Whitequiet Flora:** **Ghost-reed** (burns with a smokeless, steady flame for masterwork lamps),
  **pale-cap mushrooms** (key ingredient for salves that cure being unmoored), and **memory-amber**
  washed up around old tree roots.
- **Harmless Life:** Silent white moths that flutter in spirals above buried trinkets, and
  pale-antlered harts that watch you calmly from a distance—if you walk slowly and don't sprint,
  they lead you toward the nearest way out of the biome or toward an unlit lamp stone.

## 7. What makes generated land feel real

Procedural worlds feel fake when they look like independent dice rolls pasted onto noise ("a random
tree here, a random rock there, a camp in the middle, a cliff that starts and stops for no
reason"). They feel real when **every small detail is a consequence of the land's shape and
history**. Here are twelve concrete generator rules of thumb for Glimway:

1. **Water obeys gravity and carves its bed:** Rivers must start at high elevation (springs at
   level-1/2 cliff feet, mountain tarns, or highland bogs) and step exclusively downhill
   (`level 2 → 1 → 0`), widening as tributaries join, slowing into oxbows and reed-beds on flat
   ground, and ending in a lasting lake or the sea. Never spawn a disconnected segment of river.
2. **Rain shadows and wet toes:** The base of any cliff face (especially south- and east-facing
   ones where water seeps out) should be slightly wetter: lush ferns, moss, springs and dark soil
   at the cliff foot, while the plateau lip right above it is windier, stonier and drier.
3. **Treelines thin with height and wind:** On level 0, trees grow in full canopies; on level 1,
   they space out and shift to birch, pine and wind-bent hawthorn; on level 2, only stunted
   krummholz scrub and heather survive; level 3 summits are bare rock, lichen and cairns.
4. **Mother-trees and saplings (Clustered Ecology):** Never place trees with uniform random noise.
   Place a few old "mother" trees first, then scatter medium trees and small saplings in downwind
   arcs around them, with shade-loving mushrooms (turncaps, ferns) tucked specifically on the
   north/shady side of big trunks and boulders.
5. **Clearings form around old things:** An ancient witness tree, a standing stone, a ruined lamp
   stone or an Echo shouldn't have random bushes clipping into it. Every landmark exerts a 3–6 tile
   "respect halo" that clears scrub, flattens micro-bumps, and rings the spot with a subtle collar
   of wildflowers or worn grass.
6. **Trails and roads are lazy walkers (Contour A\*):** Generated game-trails, old carter tracks
   and ruins should never drive straight up a cliff or through deep water. Run a weighted
   pathfinder between region landmarks that strongly penalises height changes and wet ground, so
   trails naturally hug the contour of a hillside, aim for low saddles, and cross rivers only at
   shallow gravel fords.
7. **Ecotones, not hard biome lines:** When Brackenwood meets Lake Country, don't draw a line where
   oaks stop and reeds start. Interpolate over 12–20 tiles: first the oaks thin into wet meadow,
   then isolated willow clumps appear, then muddy shore, then reeds. Let small "islands" of the
   neighbouring biome scatter across the border.
8. **Geology comes in seams and erratics:** Rocks shouldn't be salt-and-pepper confetti. Spawn
   boulders in long diagonal "outcrop lines" that match the strike of the region's ridges, and
   scatter smaller talus stones exclusively within 4 tiles of a cliff base where they visibly fell
   from above.
9. **Animals and monsters live where their food and shelter are:** Goats spawn within 8 tiles of
   level-1/2 cliffs and ramps; ducks and kingfishers stay near reeds; beetles cluster near deadwood
   and resin pines; things of the Dark pool in topological hollows, sunless north-side ravines, and
   high-pressure basins where wind doesn't reach.
10. **Human ruins follow logistics:** Old company saw-pits belong near iron-oak groves and streams;
    charcoal clamps belong in birch hills; dark lamp stones and route stones always sit in an
    intelligible line pointing from one pass or ford to the next, spaced roughly a stone's throw
    apart ("never two dark in a row").
11. **Screen-space readability at cliff lips:** Because Glimway uses a 3/4 view where south-facing
    cliffs show 3 rows of rock face, keep tall canopy trees at least 2 tiles back from the *bottom*
    of a cliff face so they don't obscure the rock wall or hide a cave mouth, while letting low
    shrubs and trailing ivy hang right over the top lip.
12. **Lasting skeleton, turning skin:** Keep the Deep Measure promise tangible—if a player
    memorises "the twin waterfall below the saddle lake," that saddle, cliff and lake basin will be
    there every week forever, even when the weekly Turning shifts the berry bushes, fallen logs and
    beetle camps around its shore.

## 8. Top ten picks (ranked)

1. **The Whitequiet as a combat-free memory biome (§6):** Turning the Whitequiet into a hushed,
   bleached-vellum landscape where your lantern is the only warm colour, the danger is coming
   unmoored/lost, and the reward is rescuing trinkets and Echoes makes Glimway's signature lore
   playable like nothing else in the genre.
2. **Skylight Caverns / Cenotes on Layer −1 (§4.3):** A dark underground cave that opens into a
   sun-lit, vine-draped subterranean garden under a collapsed roof shaft gives explorers the
   ultimate "I want to build our Wilds hearth *right here*" moment.
3. **Contour-Following Trails & Saddle Passes (§2.2, §7.6):** Having generated goat-tracks,
   switchbacks and old carter roads naturally seek out low level-1 saddles through level-3 mountain
   ranges makes mountains feel like real geography you travel *through*, not just noise blobs you
   walk around.
4. **Lookout Cairns with Distant Lamp Glints (§2.4, §5.7):** Reaching a level-3 summit where the
   valley fades into fog below and seeing the real, tiny golden pinpoint of a friend's lit way-lamp
   twenty chunks away ties height, multiplayer presence and the lamp lore into one quiet, magical
   payoff.
5. **The Sapping Grounds & Amberwash (§1.3):** Pine barrens with translucent high canopies and
   sparkling gravel washes where you pan for candle-, hearth- and storm-grade amber grounds the
   game's core currency and history in a tactile, gorgeous near-home biome.
6. **Frets that Repaint with Colour When Lit (§1.10):** Rare high-pressure valleys drained to
   monochrome silver-and-ash where carrying a lantern or setting a way-lamp literally blooms warm
   colour and birdsong back into the grass inside its radius makes fighting the Dark immediately
   visual from top-down.
7. **Three-Ring Readable Lake Shores with Sunken Spits (§3.2):** Giving lasting lakes a dry willow
   bank, a wet clay-and-footprint ring, a wadeable reed fringe, and pale submerged gravel spits
   leading out to small crannog islands makes every lake an invitation to explore, fish and gather
   clay.
8. **Light-Reactive Cave Ecology & Mirror-Spar (§4.2):** Using the height lab's radial cave
   darkness as a gameplay mechanic—where Fingersnap or a set lamp flares crystal veins to reveal
   paths across underground lakes and pushes shadow-creatures to the rim—makes layer −1 spelunking
   feel cozy-adventurous rather than punishing.
9. **Cowl-Roof Ruins & Turncap Compass Rings (§5.4):** Tumbledown stone huts whose rusted chimney
   cowls and surrounding grey turncap mushrooms physically lean toward the nearest held light turn
   canon §3.9 into a readable visual compass when you're far from home.
10. **The Twelve Realism Rules for the Generator (§7):** Especially *water runs downhill*, *trees
    thin with height*, *talus only at cliff toes*, and *respect halos around landmarks*—these cheap
    geometric constraints in the generator are what turn "procedural chunks" into a countryside
    worth walking on a Tuesday evening.
