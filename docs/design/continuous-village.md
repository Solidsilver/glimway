# A continuous Hearthwick

2026-10-11. Place design for the owner, based on this worktree (`c1789ed`). Proposals below are for comparison, not new owner decisions. No source changes or playtests in this pass.

**Recommendation: A, a compact square with the Commons immediately north.** Keep the Tangle where it is. Give the opening a clear east road, reserve a real housing district west, and let a later west road reach changing country. Hearthwick should feel like people keeping one small place together beside land that will not stay put.

## 1. What the village is for

Hearthwick is the place to begin, return, find someone, do a small errand, recover, and meet friends. The square is its social and story centre; the Commons is its departure, building and neighbour centre. Outdoor continuity should make those jobs easier to understand.

Today's builders make a 42×26 village, 62×42 minimum Commons (longer with more gate rows), 56×30 Brackenwood and 34×24 Ashwatch. They are separate places, joined by portals, rather than adjoining rectangles. Both village exits face east today; rearranging the Commons north is a deliberate change to geography.

## 2. The frame for every option

**North is up, negative y. Each bracket is one 24×24-tile chunk.** Chunk (0,0) covers world tiles x=0..23, y=0..23. Tangle stays at x=-1..1, y=-2..0. Its gate is on (0,0)'s south edge, local x=1..3, y=23; the lane below must meet world x=1..3 at y=24. It is not centred at x=12.

Generated frontier land exists beside the Tangle, but **nothing is generated south of chunk row 0 today**. Every pictured village, field, plot reserve and story wood at y≥1 is proposed fixed authored ground. A future road beyond those reservations needs an explicit frontier/route change; a line in a sketch does not open generated land. Keep the existing Wilds coordinates, discoveries and paths.

Sketches show the dominant use of each chunk; buildings and paths can cross their lines. Blank cells are outside this proposal. `T` = Tangle; `C` = Commons; `Y` = Yard/office; `S` = square; `G` = green; `X` = crossroads; `H` = existing house frontage; `B` = bakery; `L` = library; `A` = Ada; `M` = mill/pond; `F` = field/orchard/verge; `W` = Brackenwood reserve; `R` = Ashwatch reserve; `P` = future housing reserve, not one home per chunk. `v` marks the real Wilds opening. ## 4. Meeting the Wilds

**A lived-in edge:** cobbles fade into trodden earth over four to six tiles; grass becomes longer beyond the ditch; fence runs end at proper posts. Use small vegetable beds, orchard trees, stacked rails and a cart-worn apron to explain why the clearing exists. Leave six to twelve tiles of verge where the fixed footprint has room. Do not build a perfectly rectangular ring of trees around every district or clear a new border strip inside stored Tangle chunks.

At the real north opening, fixed ground starts at y=24. The arch stands on its south side; the path lines up at x=1..3 without moving the opening. Fences/hedges turn away from the mouth rather than clipping it. The three-tile opening is a short pinch, widening to a four-tile town lane and a six-tile staging apron. Elara's pack/fire ring stand to the east with a clear return view. Preserve the current Tangle side; blend mainly on newly authored ground.

**Fixed is not the same as safe.** Hearthwick, public Commons/housing lanes and their maintained verges are safe; Tangle, Brackenwood and Ashwatch remain adventure ground even where fixed. Put the north safety boundary across the gate mouth, the east boundary at the last town fence before the finger-wisp, and later-road boundaries at matching hedge/ditch crossings. Carry fences or a maintained boundary verge between outlets so cutting across grass has the same legible rule. Safety must follow this visible ground, not chunk edges. Enemies cannot follow or shoot into the safe apron; a hero at zero HP stops before unsafe ground while services remain reachable.

Use one consistent cue: paired boundary posts, a narrow change from maintained to rough ground and a sign naming the destination. Warm lamp on the village side, cold/less tended ground beyond. A brief first-crossing line can say “Beyond the posts, the woods.” Colour supports the cue but is not its only meaning. A lit unsafe road is still unsafe; lamp warmth must not promise protection that the rules do not provide.

**Night:** today there is no general day/night cycle; Closure Night supplies the night tint and lit-lamp festival. Design a readable night arrangement now without adding that system. Keep the square hearth, Ada's window, library/bakery doors, Commons arch, Yard door and housing junctions as distinct warm points. Aim for sight of the next public-lane lamp, roughly 10–14 tiles away, subject to the actual glow art. Preserve the paid/dark/repair states of story and village lamps. Darkness on the story road is part of the opening, so decorative town lights stop before its first lamp.

**Turning beside fixed ground:** town, story reserve, orchards and public housing approaches remain fixed. The Tangle also remains still. Adjacent generated country can change its interior, but cannot move a fence, block a doorstep, reroute a mill-race or cut off a public lane. Mark the authored outer margin as a protected edge; changing land meets its existing openings. No new bridge/river continuity is implied: author the mill's feeder within fixed ground, and connect it to the generated Wend only with a separately designed, stable water join.

A later west/south road ends its permanent approach at one readable post pair. Past it, paths can change each wick until Lamps hold them. Turning preserves the approach and any held land; an affected player steps to nearby open ground, following the current Lamps amendment, not back to town. Show moving outer woodland beside an unmoved orchard to make permanence understandable. The town does not need a player lamp to exist or create extra low-difficulty/frontier seeds simply because it is fixed.
