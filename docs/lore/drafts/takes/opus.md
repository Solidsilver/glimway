# Opus's take: the people of Hearthwick

Focus: Mara, Pip, Orrin, Silas, Elara, Finn; who was lost in the Lost
Expedition and who is still waiting for them; how the player's help changes
those people, and how homestead visits, village projects, seasons and the
Wilds reveal them over time.

Names of places beyond the map (Sallow Ford, the Merrow Saltings, the Low
Road) are mine, and the world writers can rename them. What matters here is
the job each place does for the people.

---

## 0. The pitch

Thirty years ago, during the lull between the Two Storms, six people from
Hearthwick went out under the closure mark to fetch lamp oil so their keeper
could reopen the road. Two of them died in the Tangle. Four reached the far
end. The keeper who closed the road learned within three years that some of
them were alive, and she told almost nobody.

The Lost Expedition stops being "what happened to them?" and becomes "what do
you do with news that is thirty years late?" Every lantern the player lights
removes a little more of the reason the keeper gave for staying silent. The
people in the village, not the woods, are what the mystery turns out to be
about.

---

## 1. What really happened (the people's timeline)

Hearthwick doesn't say "the Lost Expedition". It says **the Lull Run**, or
**when the Six went**. The game's journal can use "Lost Expedition" as the
player's own label until a villager corrects them.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Before | The Lantern Road runs east through the Wilds to **Sallow Ford**, a river-crossing town that presses lamp oil from sallow-seed. Hearthwick sends iron-oak and amber east and gets oil back. **Wenna Hale** is the keeper, **Nan Greer** her lamplighter, **Hollis Brack** the lead carter, and **Orrin** builds the bridges. |
| The first storm | The Wilds drift hard. Lanterns burn through their oil defending the road. Wenna's ledger: *"Oil, 3 casks short."* She can't keep the road lit, so she closes it and cuts closure marks: two weaves and a break. |
| The lull | Nine days of slack calm. Hollis argues that nine days is enough to get to Sallow Ford and back with oil. Wenna says no. Six go anyway, at first light on the sixth day: Hollis, the twins **Tam and Bett Cooley**, Orrin's apprentice **Dorrit Venn**, the sixteen-year-old runner **Joss Penhallow**, and Nan Greer. **Silas**, Hollis's husband, was meant to be the seventh. He turned back at the Brackenwood fork. |
| The second storm | It comes three days early. At the Tangle crossing, Orrin's bridge drifts out from under the Cooley cart. **Bett** and **Dorrit** are lost. The other four push on and reach Sallow Ford, but the road behind them has already rearranged itself, and every lantern on it is dark. |
| Spring after | **Wenna and Orrin build the stone warden** on the Ashwatch path in eleven days, to stop the next party. In the second spring it turns back exactly one person: Silas. |
| Third autumn | A carved fox comes down the river Wend and catches in the mill grate. Its long ear is on the right. Hollis carved them that way. The miller, **Aldo Tolley**, brings it to Wenna. She asks him to keep it, and to keep quiet. |
| Every autumn since | Another fox. Aldo keeps them in an oak box, tagged by year. Wenna adds a tally mark on the last page of her ledger, written in keeper's marks so nobody else can read it. |
| ~8 years ago | Wenna dies. Mara, then 19, inherits the ledger and the closed road. She can't read the last page. |
| Two winters ago | Aldo dies. His son **Finn** finds the box, along with a note: *"For Wenna. Not for anyone else. She'll say when."* |
| Now | The player arrives by the Low Road, the slow western valley road that the carters still use (which is how "the carters mentioned" the traveler). There are twenty-seven foxes in the box. |

**Why Wenna kept quiet.** Her ledger reasons are on the record, and they are
human, not villainous:

- *"A fox is not a man. Hope is not a road."* A fox proves that somebody
  carved it, once, somewhere upriver.
- If Hearthwick learned the Six were alive, half the village would walk into
  the Wilds after them, and the road couldn't be held. She'd already lost two
  people to a road she couldn't keep lit.
- The person with the most right to know, Silas, had told her in the second
  spring: *"If you've news, I don't want it unless it's walking."* She took
  him at his word for the rest of her life.

What the player changes: the road becomes holdable. Every lantern lit is one
fewer of Wenna's reasons still standing.

## 2. Fixing the warden: how a village makes stone that moves

Owner's decision: Wenna and Orrin built it. The mechanism has to come from
things the world already has:

1. **Drift-stone.** Stones from the dark Wilds drift the same way trees do,
   moving a hand's width at a time while nobody watches. Hearthwick children
   know you never build a wall out of Wilds stone.
2. **The frame** is iron-oak: the unfinished second span of the Tangle bridge,
   Dorrit's last job. Orrin cut it up for the warden and has never said so.
3. **The heart-lamp.** A small lantern in its chest that burns a fist of
   amber, which burns for decades. A lantern holds whatever is in its light,
   so a small enough light holds a small enough thing: the warden itself.
4. **The pose.** They stood the stones in the shape they wanted, arms across
   the path, and kept watch for a full night with the heart-lamp burning, so
   the stone learned that shape. After that, whenever drift or a person pushes
   it, it walks back to that pose.

It isn't alive and it isn't guarding anything. It's **homesick for one
position**. To anyone walking toward the shrine, that looks like being
blocked.

**Why the rubbing matters.** Orrin cut the closure mark into its chest plate.
The "break" in two weaves and a break is a real gap: the latch of the hatch
over the heart-lamp. Anyone who has read the mark knows where to reach. That
gives the existing quest a literal key, and it fits Orrin's "the road wanted
people who paid attention." In the fight, you push the warden off balance
until you can reach the break. When the hatch opens, the amber, which is
nearly spent anyway, gutters, and the warden settles into a sitting heap.

> **Orrin:** "I wasn't going to build a thing nobody could ever open. I'm not cruel. I'm thorough."

Late in the game, warden stone becomes the solution to Orrin's own joke about
"a bridge that moves" (section 7, North Bridge).

## 3. My twists

### Twist 1: the Lost Expedition mostly lived, and the keeper knew

The obvious versions are that they died (sad, closed), that they became ghosts
or echoes (draft 1's "phantom camps... integrated into the woods", which is
vague and takes their agency away), or that some cosmic woods-force swallowed
them. All three make the mystery about the Wilds.

My version makes it about **people deciding what to do with the truth**:

- It gives each living NPC a different stake in one secret. Finn holds the
  evidence. Mara holds a page she can't read. Silas is the one most wronged by
  the silence, and partly asked for it. Orrin's bridge is where the two deaths
  happened. Pip's uncle is out there. Old Ada never stopped expecting them.
- It's bittersweet in the right proportion. Bett and Dorrit really are dead,
  so the grief is earned. The others have lived thirty years without
  Hearthwick: married, had children, grown old. A reunion would be with
  strangers who share your name.
- **The player's progress is the moral pivot.** Wenna was right to stay
  silent when the road couldn't be held. The player is what makes it holdable.
  Nobody has to be a villain for the story to turn.
- **The far end has been waiting too.** Hollis has kept a lantern burning at
  Sallow Ford's end of the road for thirty years, the twin of Ada's window lamp
  at this end. The endgame image: the player lights enough of the chain that,
  from the last hill, **a point of gold appears that isn't ours**.

### Twist 2: Elara is somebody else's Lost Expedition

The obvious Elara is a botanist who came to study the woods. Mine drifted in.
Two summers ago she was foraging in the **Merrow Saltings**, a fen country
far to the south-west where the woods drift slowly. A spring flood-drift
carried her for three days, and she walked out at the edge of Hearthwick. She
doesn't know the way back, she's too proud to say so, and she has called it
"fieldwork" ever since.

Why this beats the obvious version:

- It mirrors the Six from the outside. Hearthwick has people stranded out
  there; Elara is someone else's stranded person in here. When she finally
  says "somebody at home is keeping a light for me," the village recognizes
  itself.
- It brings in a culture from beyond the map with the opposite philosophy.
  Hearthwick fights the drift with anchors; the Saltings go along with it.
  Saltings people keep **drift-tables**, like tide tables for the woods: when
  the woods go "on the flood", when there's "slack water". Elara calls
  Hearthwick's lanterns "nailing the tablecloth down." Neither side is wrong,
  so the world gets a debate without a villain.
- It closes a historical loop. Any Saltings child could have told Hollis that
  slack water never lasts nine days in a two-storm year. If Hearthwick had
  known what the Saltings know, the Six wouldn't have gone.
- It gives a system a diegetic reason. The design says outer-Wilds resets are
  announced on the notice board a day ahead. **Elara writes those notices**,
  from her drift-tables.

### Small twist: the mirror-image foxes

Silas learned to carve from Hollis while they sat facing each other on the
cart bench, so he carves everything mirror-wise. **Silas's foxes have the long
ear on the left; Hollis's have it on the right.** Pip collects foxes (runners
get paid in them) and is the first to notice that a fox found in the Wilds is
made of fresh pine, carved the wrong way round, and isn't Silas's.

## 4. The Six

Each of the Six gets a fate, someone who still remembers them at home, and an
object or place in the Wilds that points back to them.

| Name | Who they were | Fate | Who remembers them in Hearthwick | What they left in the Wilds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hollis Brack** | Lead carter, 41. Laughed with his whole back. Carved a fox for every door he delivered to, so the house would remember him. | Reached Sallow Ford. Alive at 71. Keeps the far-end lantern lit. Sends a fox down the Wend every autumn. | **Silas**, his husband, who believes he's dead. | *The Wrong-Ear Fox* (trinket) and *Hollis's Camp* (POI). |
| **Tam Cooley** | Ox-driver, 34. Bett's twin. Talked to the oxen more than to people. | Reached Sallow Ford. Alive at 64, married there, two grown children. | **Ada Cooley**, his mother, 88. | Tam's knotted ox-halter. |
| **Bett Cooley** | Tam's twin. Drove the second cart. Sang flat on purpose to annoy Tam. | Lost at the Tangle crossing when the cart went over. | **Ada**, who doesn't know which twin lived. | *The Cooley Cart* (the "Abandoned Cart" POI). |
| **Dorrit Venn** | Orrin's apprentice, 20, from down the Low Road. Set the village signpost on her first day, crooked. | Lost at the Tangle crossing, trying to hold the bridge chain. | **Orrin**, the only one left who really remembers her. | *Dorrit's Plumb Bob* (trinket) and her glove in the *Frozen Pond*. |
| **Joss Penhallow** | Runner, 16. Cut notches at every fork. His whistle was dented to a note you could pick out across the square. | Reached Sallow Ford. Alive at 46, the town's mapmaker, still trying to chart a way back. | **Hazel Penhallow**, his sister, now the baker and Pip's mum. | *Runner's Notches* (POI) and *The Penhallow Whistle* (trinket). |
| **Nan Greer** | Wenna's lamplighter, in her fifties. Read route marks the way other people read faces. | Reached Sallow Ford. Died there of old age eleven years ago. | Nobody close; Wenna was her closest friend. | *Nan's Chalk Weaves* (POI): trail marks pointing *home*, cut during the second storm. |

The four survivors left traces too. In the first years at Sallow Ford, Joss
and Nan tried to mark a way back, and some of those marks drift into the
generated Wilds. Nan's chalk weaves point toward Hearthwick, not away from it.
Players who learn to read marks notice that before anyone in the village says
a word.

## 5. The people now

Two rules for every Hearthwick voice:

- **Nobody counts your days.** This is a game fed by real-life progress.
  NPCs never scold an absence, never mention how long the player was away in
  a guilty way, and never react to a gap with anything but warmth. "There you
  are" is the most anyone says. (Ada has spent thirty years showing what
  waiting without blame looks like.)
- **Relationship stages show up in the words people use**, not in a meter.
  The village's own scale: *nodding terms → tea terms → spare-key terms*.
  Each NPC has a verbal tell that changes as you move up (Mara's is the
  clearest: see below).

### Mara Hale — keeper of the ledger, 27

**Backstory.** Her parents went down the Low Road for work when she was six
and left her with her grandmother, Wenna, "for a season". She stayed. She grew
up in the Keeper's House at the top of the square, under a lamp-loft she
still doesn't go into. She grew up watching half the village treat Wenna as
the woman who let the Six go, and the other half as the woman who closed the
road on them. It's never quite been forgiven either way, and Mara has spent
her life being so reliable that nobody gets to say anything about the Hales.

**Want.** For the road to be lit *as Wenna's road*, so that her grandmother
is remembered as the keeper who kept it, not the one who closed it.

**Fear.** That it was easy all along: if a traveler can relight the road in a
season, why did Wenna leave it dark for thirty years?

**Secret.** She has the last page of the ledger: a column of tally marks and
lines in keeper's marks she can't read. She has never shown it to anyone,
because she's afraid it says Wenna did something wrong. (It says Wenna did
something hard.)

**Habits and objects.** The ledger lives in green oilcloth. She writes in
pencil and keeps ink "for what's done". She says *"Noted."* when she
disagrees, a habit she doesn't know she got from Wenna's ledger. She runs the
**ledger soup**, a pot in the square for anyone working on a village project;
"soup's on the ledger" means the village is paying. Thursday is bone-broth
day.

**Voice.** Brisk and warm. Lists things in threes. Dry jokes at her own
expense. Never says "she would have wanted".

**Her tell, the name she uses:**
- **nodding terms:** "my grandmother"
- **tea terms:** "Gran"
- **after the last page is read:** "Wenna", because she finally sees her
  grandmother as a person who made a choice.

**Arc beats.**
1. *Curated quest:* as written. Hopeful, a little proud.
2. *Lantern lit:* relief, with a crack showing. "Thirty years. One traveler."
3. *Tea terms* (after you contribute to the first village project): she talks
   about Wenna as a person, not a legacy: Gran's sayings, Gran's temper.
4. *Lamp-loft project* (section 7): cleaning out the loft turns up Wenna's
   primer of keeper's marks. Mara can read the last page now, and she can't
   make herself do it alone.
5. *Spare-key terms:* she reads the last page with the player in the loft.
   Twenty-seven tallies. *"A fox is not a man. Hope is not a road."*
6. *After:* she tells Finn she knows, and together they decide how to tell
   Silas. She starts lighting the loft lamp again: Hearthwick's first keeper
   in thirty years, by choice and not by inheritance.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Mara** · idle, nodding: "Twelve casks of oil, four of barley, one of whatever Orrin calls varnish. Noted, noted, and I'd rather not."
- **Mara** · lantern-lit: "Thirty years that hill was dark. One traveler, one season. I'm glad. I am. I just need to sit down for a minute."
- **Mara** · tea terms: "Gran used to say a closed road is still a road. You just owe it more attention. She paid it plenty, believe me."
- **Mara** · tea terms, soup: "Soup's on the ledger. Leek and barley. If Orrin tells you it needs salt, it doesn't. He just says that."
- **Mara** · at the lamp-loft door: "I haven't been up there since the funeral. It smells like her. Lamp-black and peppermint. Come up with me?"
- **Mara** · after the last page: "Wenna knew. Twenty-seven autumns, and she knew. I'm not angry yet. I think I'm going to be. Then I think I'll understand."
- **Mara** · after the last page: "She wrote 'noted' in the margin by Silas's name. That's what I say when I disagree and won't argue. I got it from her."
- **Mara** · welcome back: "There you are. The pot's still warm. It's always still warm. That's rather the point of a pot."

### Pip Penhallow — runner, 12 (they/them)

**Backstory.** Hazel the baker's child. They run messages between the square
and the mill, "the fastest job in the world and also the only one." Runners
in Hearthwick get paid in whatever's handy, and the whole village knows Pip's
rate is one whittled fox. Pip has a shelf of Silas's foxes and can tell you
which year each one was carved.

**Want.** To run a real road. The first runner on the lantern road since
"someone" (their mum won't say who). They keep the **Pencil Map**, every line
dated, because the Wilds won't stay inked.

**Secret.** Draft 1 made Pip secretly afraid of the dark. I'd drop that: it's
a stock trait. Pip's secret is that they *aren't* afraid, but Hazel is. Pip
has been slipping out at dawn to follow old **runner's notches** into the edge
of the Tangle. (The existing line about "a fallen oak with a notch cut in it"
is one of Joss's.) Pip doesn't know the notches are their uncle's.

**What Pip doesn't know.** That Joss was their uncle. Hazel calls him "my
brother" when she mentions him at all, which is almost never.

**The whistle.** Every Hearthwick runner dents their tin whistle until it
plays a note that's theirs. Pip's is still undented: they've never found
their note. The *Penhallow Whistle* the player finds in the Wilds plays a
note Hazel hears from the bakery, and she drops a tray.

**Voice.** Bursts. Counts everything ("nine people"). Grand words used
slightly wrong ("scientific cartography"). Corrects themselves mid-sentence.
Hates being called a child.

**Tell:** on nodding terms Pip brags; on tea terms Pip shows you the Pencil
Map; on spare-key terms Pip admits something they don't know.

**Arc beats.**
1. Curated quest as written.
2. *Inner Wilds open:* Pip asks you to check whether their notches are
   "real or just woodpeckers."
3. *You find the Wrong-Ear Fox:* Pip spots the ear, then insists on bringing
   it to Silas, then loses their nerve and asks you to do it.
4. *You find the Penhallow Whistle:* visit Hazel's bakery; Pip learns about
   Joss.
5. *Resolution:* Pip doesn't dent their whistle to match Joss's. They pick
   their own note, which is the point of the custom. Pip becomes the runner
   on the relit road, carrying notes as far as the lanterns reach.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Pip** · idle: "I counted the lanterns from the mill roof. Three. Well, two and a firefly. Two and a half. I'm rounding up for morale."
- **Pip** · tea terms: "This is the Pencil Map. Pencil because the Tangle moves. Every line has a date. It's not wrong, it's historical."
- **Pip** · notches quest: "There's a notch on every fork out there. Same cut, same height. Woodpeckers can't count. Someone was marking a route."
- **Pip** · wrong-ear fox: "This isn't Silas's. Silas does the long ear on the left. Always. Who carves a fox backwards? And it's new pine. It's new."
- **Pip** · wrong-ear fox: "You give it to him. I'll be right behind you. Some distance behind you. In the mill, probably."
- **Pip** · after the whistle: "Mum had a brother. Joss. He was a runner. He had a note. Nobody told me I had a — I'm not crying, it's flour."
- **Pip** · resolution: "I'm not denting mine like his. His note's his. I'm going to find mine. Probably somewhere around 'loud'."
- **Pip** · welcome back: "You're back! I ran past your place twice to check. Not because I was worried. For cartography."

### Orrin — bridge builder, sixty-odd

**Backstory.** He's built every bridge within a day of Hearthwick, and the
Tangle crossing on the Lantern Road was his best work. In the second storm it
drifted out from under the Cooley cart, while his apprentice Dorrit was
holding the chain. He built the warden with Wenna the next spring, from
Dorrit's unfinished second span. Then he built nothing in the Wilds for
thirty years.

**Want.** Things that stay where they're put. That isn't fussiness: a bridge
that won't stay where it's put kills people.

**Secret(s).**
- The signpost doesn't lean because of frost. **Dorrit set it on her first
  day, crooked**, and he has never straightened it. Every winter he resets
  the post, and he resets it to the same lean.
- The warden's frame is Dorrit's bridge.
- In the second spring, the warden stopped Silas. Orrin watched it happen
  from the ridge, and the two of them have never talked about it. "Orrin
  knows nothing about joints" is what Silas says instead.

**Habits and objects.** He measures in fingers ("three fingers off plumb").
He keeps a boxwood folding rule with brass hinges in his apron and never lets
anyone borrow it; it has *D.V.* scratched on the back. "Hm." "Aye." Every
complaint ends in a grudging concession.

**Voice.** Short declaratives. Complains, then concedes. He never says
Dorrit's name until spare-key terms; before that she's "my last apprentice."

**Tell:** his complaints shrink as you move up. On spare-key terms he asks
you for something, which he has never done.

**Arc beats.**
1. *Curated quest.* Keep his warden reveal **gradual**. In `accepted` he says
   the warden is stone and dutiful, not that he built it. In `clue-found` he
   reads the mark. After `guardian-defeated` comes a pause: "I'll go up
   tomorrow and look at it." He admits building it on tea terms.
2. *Well canopy:* small, easy work. He cuts the first new **direction mark**
   in thirty years into its lintel. It means "here".
3. *Frozen Pond / Dorrit's Plumb Bob found:* he says her name.
4. *North Bridge project:* he works out that the warden trick (drift-stone
   that remembers its pose, plus a heart-lamp) makes **a bridge that walks
   back to its place after drift**. The thing that stopped people becomes the
   thing that carries them.
5. *Resolution:* he hangs Dorrit's plumb bob from the leaning signpost. The
   post still leans, and the bob hangs true beside it. That's the visible
   repair.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Orrin** · idle: "Signpost's three fingers off plumb. Frost. Don't look at me like that. I said frost."
- **Orrin** · after warden: "Thirty years it stood. I'll go up tomorrow and look at it. Not to fuss. To look. A man can look at his own work."
- **Orrin** · tea terms: "Aye, I built it. Wenna and me, eleven days. Drift-stone, amber heart. It isn't angry. It's homesick for one shape."
- **Orrin** · tea terms: "A lantern holds what's in its light. Small light, small thing. That's all the warden is. A small thing held very hard."
- **Orrin** · Dorrit's plumb bob: "That's Dorrit's. Dorrit Venn. My last apprentice. She set that signpost her first day. Crooked as a dog's hind leg."
- **Orrin** · Dorrit's plumb bob: "I'm not straightening it. I reset it every spring, same lean. Don't tell Mara. She'll want to make it a thing."
- **Orrin** · north bridge: "Warden stone wants to go home to one shape. So give it a bridge shape. Let the Wilds shove it. It'll walk back by morning."
- **Orrin** · welcome back: "Hm. You're back. Good. Nobody else here holds the end of a plank properly."

### Silas — retired carter, homestead builder, 64

**Backstory.** He drove the Lantern Road for twenty years beside Hollis.
They married in the square under the old lamp post. He was meant to drive the
seventh cart on the Lull Run. At the Brackenwood fork he turned back. He told
everyone his knee went. **It didn't.** He was frightened: he heard the wind
change and knew, and Hollis didn't. He has limped on his left knee ever since,
partly from habit, mostly so nobody would ask.

In the second spring he walked out alone to follow them, and the warden
turned him back. He hasn't climbed to Ashwatch since.

He carves foxes for every new door in Hearthwick, the way Hollis did:
**door-foxes**, set over the lintel "so the house remembers you". His come out
mirror-wise, long ear on the left.

**Want.** To build homes for other people. It's the closest he can get to
having one himself. "A house is just a box until someone lights a fire in it"
is draft 1's line, and a good one; it's really about him.

**Secret.** The knee. And the sentence he said to Wenna: *"If you've news, I
don't want it unless it's walking."*

**Habits and objects.** A pocketknife with a horn handle, and pine shavings in
his cuffs. He calls everyone "neighbour". Cart talk leaks into his building
talk ("she'll pull left until the roof settles"). He forgets to limp when
he's absorbed in a joint, and Elara notices before anyone else does.

**Voice.** Slow and measured. Lets silences sit. Talks about wood when he
means people.

**Tell:** he says "Hollis" exactly once before the reveal, by accident, on
spare-key terms. Before that, it's "my old partner", then "my Hollis".

**Arc beats.**
1. *Commons, tier 0:* first meeting. Fix the contradiction: he drove the
   road for years and was meant to go with the Six, and didn't.
2. *Cottage (tier 1):* he sets a door-fox over your lintel, facing east,
   without explaining why.
3. *Warden defeated:* he isn't at his bench for a day. His chisels are laid
   out in a neat row, as if for inspection. He went up the hill.
4. *Spare-key terms:* he tells you about the knee.
5. *The reveal (after Mara and Finn decide):* the box is opened in his
   workshop. He picks up one fox, turns it, and says nothing for a long
   time. Then: "He did the ears wrong on purpose, you know. So I'd know."
6. *After:* no instant reunion. He starts carving a fox with **both** ears
   long, to send *upriver*, and asks the player how far the lanterns reach
   now.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Silas** · first meeting: "Well now, a neighbour. I drove the lantern road twenty years. Was meant to drive the last run, too. Didn't. Now I build roofs."
- **Silas** · idle: "Measure twice, cut once, and complain about the saw. Orrin skips the first two."
- **Silas** · door-fox: "Every door in Hearthwick gets a fox over it. So the house remembers you. Old carter's habit. Not mine. I just kept it."
- **Silas** · after warden: "Went up the hill yesterday. It was sitting down. Thirty years I've been angry at a heap of stones that was only doing as told."
- **Silas** · spare-key terms: "The knee was fine. It's always been fine. I heard the wind turn at the fork, and I was scared, and Hollis wasn't."
- **Silas** · the reveal: "He did the ears backwards on purpose, you know. So I'd know it was him. Twenty-seven of them. I said I didn't want news."
- **Silas** · after: "Both ears long. That way he'll know it's both of us asking. How far do your lanterns reach now, neighbour?"
- **Silas** · welcome back: "There's a pot of glue going and a kettle going. One of them's for you. I'll let you guess which."

### Elara Quill — forager, 30s, from the Merrow Saltings

**Backstory.** In the Saltings, the woods drift slowly and people farm the
drift like a tide. Elara is a **drift-forager**: she gathers plants that only
grow at the drift's edges. Two summers ago a spring flood-drift carried her
three days' walk from anywhere she knew. She came out of the trees at the
Hearthwick Commons with a satchel of specimens, introduced herself as a
naturalist "doing fieldwork", and set up the neatest camp anyone has ever
seen on the Commons edge: stakes, coloured thread, labelled jars.

**Want (stated).** To prove the Wilds' drift has tides: direction, timing,
pattern. **Want (real).** To get home without having to admit she's lost.

**Secret.** Stranded. Someone in the Saltings, her sister Bryony, keeps a
light in a window for her, and she knows it.

**What she discovers that matters to everyone:**
- **Turncaps**, small grey mushrooms whose caps always face the nearest lit
  lantern. They're useful for the player in the Wilds. In the outer Wilds,
  some turncaps face *east*, toward no lantern Hearthwick knows: **something
  out there has been lit for years.** That's the first physical sign of
  Hollis's far lamp.
- **Keeper's moss** grows only on stone that's been in lantern light for a
  long time. Moss on a route stone tells you how long the road was lit.
- **Loiterberries** are Pip's red soap-tasting berries. They ripen only where
  the drift is slack.
- She can predict outer-Wilds reshuffles a day ahead, and she posts the
  notices.

**Voice.** Quick and precise. Uses Saltings words: *the drift, on the flood,
slack water, nailing the tablecloth down.* Talks to plants by their full
invented names. Calls the player "colleague". **Never says "home."** She says
"the Saltings" or "where I'm from"; when "home" slips out, that's her tell.

**Relationships.** She argues with Finn about the Wend like an old married
couple, which they are not (nothing more than that unless the owner wants
it). She spotted Silas forgetting his limp in her first week and never said a
word, which he noticed. She accepts Mara's soup only as "data rations".

**Arc beats.**
1. *First meeting* (inner Wilds open): she's busy, recruits you as a
   "colleague", and asks you to check her stakes after the next shift.
2. *Seasons:* she posts drift notices. She explains drift-tables if you ask.
3. *Turncaps facing east:* she brings it to you, not to the village. "I don't
   want to give anyone hope I can't measure."
4. *Spare-key terms:* she says "home" by accident, and then tells the truth.
5. *Resolution:* when the chain reaches far enough east that the far light
   is visible, she can get from Sallow Ford down the river road to the
   Saltings. Whether she goes is the owner's call. My recommendation: she
   sends a letter home with the first runner, and stays one more season
   "to finish the tables". She's choosing now, not stuck.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Elara** · first meeting: "Don't step on the thread. That's three weeks of drift on one string. You're a colleague now, I suppose. Hold this jar."
- **Elara** · idle: "Your village nails the tablecloth down with lanterns. Where I'm from we just learn when it's going to be pulled."
- **Elara** · drift lore: "The drift has tides. On the flood, everything walks. Slack water, it sits. Nobody here keeps tables. Astonishing."
- **Elara** · on the Six: "Nine days of slack in a two-storm year? Any Saltings child would've told them. Three, at best. I'm sorry. Truly."
- **Elara** · turncaps: "Turncaps face the nearest lit lantern. Always. These face east. There's no lantern east. Is there? Should there be?"
- **Elara** · the slip: "Once the eastern chain is lit I could be home by — oh. I said it. Don't look at me like that. Fine. Sit down."
- **Elara** · confession: "I didn't come to study the drift. It brought me. Two summers. My sister keeps a lamp in our window. I know she does."
- **Elara** · on Silas: "He forgets to limp when he's fitting a joint. I've never mentioned it. Some things you leave on the stake."

### Finn Tolley — miller, 36

**Backstory.** He was six during the Lull Run. He grew up at the mill on the
Wend, a river that runs out of the Wilds and wanders when the Wilds drift:
three feet one night, a yard back the next. His father, Aldo, kept the mill
running through all of it. Aldo died two winters ago, and Finn found the oak
box, which smells of linseed: twenty-seven pine foxes wrapped in mill sacking,
each tagged with a year in Aldo's square hand, and the note: *"For Wenna. Not
for anyone else. She'll say when."*

**Want.** A river that stays put. A wheel that turns forty-one times a
minute. And to be **let off a promise** made by two dead people.

**Fear.** That he's the last link in a chain of silence, and that by saying
nothing he's choosing the same thing his father chose.

**Habits and objects.** Counts wheel turns under his breath. "Forty-one and
holding" means a good day. There's flour in his eyebrows and a carpenter's
pencil behind each ear because he keeps forgetting he has one. He names the
Wend's moods like weather: *sulking, wandering, sitting up*. The mill sign
has the same curly marks Pip noticed: Aldo was a keeper's friend and painted
route marks on everything.

**Voice.** Anxious, apologetic, numerate. Long worried run-ons that end in a
short practical fix.

**Tell:** on nodding terms he apologizes; on tea terms he complains; on
spare-key terms he asks a question he's been holding for two years.

**Why he tells the player first.** The player is the one person in Hearthwick
the silence was never about. Then the two of them take it to Mara, because
the note says "for Wenna" and Mara is the nearest thing left.

**Arc beats.**
1. *Mill wheel project:* the wheel is lashed and steadier, and Finn is on
   tea terms.
2. *First autumn with an eastern lantern lit:* the Wend wanders less. Finn
   waits at the grate on the first frost, and the player can be there.
3. *The fox comes down.* He shows you the box.
4. *With Mara:* they find out together what the tally marks on the last page
   mean.
5. *Resolution:* "Forty-one and holding" stops being about the wheel.

**Sample lines (≤160):**
- **Finn** · idle: "The Wend wandered three feet west last night. The wheel's groaning. Forty — thirty-eight. Thirty-eight and complaining."
- **Finn** · idle: "Sorry. Sorry, I'm counting. If I stop counting it gets worse. It doesn't, actually. But it feels like it will."
- **Finn** · mill wheel done: "Forty-one and holding. Forty-one! I don't know what to do with my hands. I'll count something else."
- **Finn** · autumn: "First frost. Dad used to come down to the grate every first frost. I never asked why. I wish I'd asked. Walk down with me?"
- **Finn** · the box: "Twenty-seven. One every autumn. Dad tagged every one. 'For Wenna. She'll say when.' She never said. They're both gone."
- **Finn** · the box: "I'm the only one left holding a promise to nobody. Is it still a promise, if there's nobody to keep it for?"
- **Finn** · on Elara: "She says the Wend has a timetable. Rivers don't have timetables. ...It was right about Tuesday. Don't tell her."
- **Finn** · resolution: "Forty-one and holding. I don't just mean the wheel."

### Supporting cast (background NPCs, notice-board voices, visitors)

- **Ada Cooley, 88.** Mother of the twins. She has kept **a lamp in her
  window every night for thirty years**, the only light in Hearthwick that
  never went out. The village pays her oil: Wenna wrote it into the ledger
  (*"Ada's lamp: 1 pint, weekly, on the village. For as long as she wants
  it."*), and Mara has kept paying. Local saying: **"leave one for Ada"**,
  meaning leave a light on for someone who's late. She never grieved; she
  just waited. When the truth comes out, one twin lived and one didn't, and
  her answer is: "I know. I never put it out, did I?" One mother waited
  correctly and lost a child anyway.
  - **Ada:** "Sit, sit. Mind the lamp. Thirty years it's been lit. My boy and girl will want to see the way in, when they come."
  - **Ada:** "People say I'm waiting. I'm not waiting, dear. I'm keeping a lamp. Waiting is what you do when you've stopped doing anything."
- **Hazel Penhallow, 40.** Baker, Pip's mum, Joss's sister. Flour to the
  elbows. Won't let Pip past the gate after dusk and won't say why. She bakes
  **keeper's twists**, a plaited bun with a glazed knot on top, which were
  sold on the Lantern Road and haven't been baked since. She starts baking
  them again the morning after the hilltop lantern is lit, without a word to
  anyone.
  - **Hazel:** "Keeper's twists. Haven't made these since I was ten. Hands remembered. Funny what hands remember."
- **Wenna Hale (absent).** Known only through her ledger, which the journal
  quotes. Sharp, practical, funny in the margins. She has the best lines of
  anyone and never appears.
- **Aldo Tolley (absent).** Finn's dad. Painted route marks on everything.
  His box tags are the most-read handwriting in the game.

## 7. How the systems reveal the people

### The reveal ladder

| Step | System | What the player learns |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Curated quest | The road was closed on purpose. A warden stands on the path. |
| 2 | Commons, tiers 0–1 | Door-foxes. Silas drove the road. Ada's lamp. The phrase "the Six" turns up in idle lines. |
| 3 | Inner Wilds POIs | The Cooley Cart, the Frozen Pond, runner's notches: the Six were real people with names. |
| 4 | Tea terms + well canopy | Orrin built the warden. Who the Six were. "Leave one for Ada." |
| 5 | Workshop tier + mill wheel | Silas's knee. Finn's counting. Dorrit's name. |
| 6 | Outer Wilds + seasons | Nan's chalk weaves point *home*. Turncaps face east. The Wrong-Ear Fox. |
| 7 | Autumn + lamp-loft | Finn's box, the last page. Wenna knew. |
| 8 | North Bridge + eastern lanterns | Silas is told. A light that isn't ours. |

Steps 6 and 7 can arrive in either order. A player who finds the Wrong-Ear
Fox before autumn knows *something* before the village does, and Pip makes
them promise not to tell. That small secret of the player's own is good: it
makes the player part of the village's habit of keeping quiet.

### Homestead visits

NPCs drop by the player's plot at each tier. Visits are one-time scenes; after
each one, that NPC gets a new idle line about your house.

| Tier | Visitor | What they bring | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Campsite | **Pip**, at dusk | A hand-drawn map of the Commons with your plot circled twice | The Commons is the furthest past the gate Pip's allowed after dark, and they're delighted. |
| 1 Cottage | **Silas** | A door-fox, set over your lintel facing east | The custom, Hollis (unnamed), the long left ear. |
| 1 Cottage | **Mara** | First-fire bread and a ledger page with your name in pencil | "Pencil until it's done." She'll ink it when you've been here a year (real calendar). |
| 2 Workshop | **Orrin** | Criticism of your bench joints, and a folding rule he *doesn't* lend you | Dorrit, unnamed: "My last apprentice had a bench like this." |
| 2 Workshop | **Silas** | Looks at your storage chest: "Built like a cart bed." | The cart bench, facing Hollis. Mirror-carving. |
| 3 Garden | **Elara** | A drift-seed for your garden, which moves one tile each night | Drift is gentle and patterned. She talks about the Saltings with "where I'm from". |
| 3 Garden | **Finn** | Seed grain "from a good year" | Finn on tea terms; the year on the sack is a tally year. |
| 4 Hall | **Ada**, carried in a chair by Orrin and Silas *together* | She asks for a lamp in your window "for anyone late" | The village's way of waiting, now including you. |

### Seasons

The season shift is a natural clock for people beats as well as for the
outer Wilds.
- **Autumn:** the fox comes down the Wend at first frost (Finn's beat).
  Before the reveal, Finn is "busy at the grate", every year.
- **Winter:** the anniversary of the Lull Run. Ada's lamp is turned up
  higher. Nobody in the square says why.
- **Spring:** Orrin resets the signpost to the same lean. Silas's bad week
  (the second spring).
- **Summer:** Elara's anniversary. She's short-tempered and won't say why.

### Fallen-hero lanterns: give the mechanic a custom

Since the Six, nobody in Hearthwick walks past a cold lantern. Relighting a
dropped one is "leaving one for Ada". Proposed text:
- *leftBehind:* "A cold lantern lies here, dropped by a traveler who stumbled in the dark. In Hearthwick, nobody walks past one of these."
- *relitByFriend:* "You spark the wick. Left one for Ada, as they say. The light will find its owner."
- *yourOwnLantern:* "Your own lantern, waiting where you fell. Someone would have lit it for you. Best pick it up first."

## 9. Disagreements with draft 1

1. **Who closed the road and who built the warden contradicts itself.** The
   draft's timeline has Wenna and *Silas* closing the road and "instructing"
   the warden, while its flagged change has Wenna and *Orrin* building it, and
   its Silas profile puts him on the expedition. Fix: Wenna closed the road
   alone (it was her road); Wenna and Orrin built the warden the spring
   after; Silas was meant to go with the Six and turned back.
2. **The rewrite has Silas "drive the last cart out of the Tangle".** That
   reads as the opposite of turning back, and it gives the reveal away in his
   first line. Fix: "Was meant to drive the last run, too. Didn't."
3. **"Carters who refused to heed the closure mark"** (rewritten Commons
   description). Ruts can't tell you who made them or why, and the phrase
   passes judgment on the Six in the narrator's voice. The village is divided
   on the Six, and the narration shouldn't pick a side. Keep the original
   neutral line; let "under the mark" carry the ambivalence in dialogue.
4. **Echoes and phantom camps "integrated into the shifting woods."**
   Ghostly, vague, and it removes the Six as people. Replace with physical
   traces (chalk weaves, notches, Hollis's camp, the winter-only Lull Camp)
   that real people left for real reasons.
5. **"The lanterns were fighting the natural flow, causing pressure."** That
   makes the player's whole project harmful. Wenna closed the road because she
   couldn't keep it lit (oil, keepers, two storms), not because anchors are
   wrong. The Saltings give the world its counter-philosophy without making
   the lanterns villainous. (The cosmology belongs to the world writers; I'm
   flagging the conflict with the people's story.)
6. **Orrin admits building the warden too early** (rewrite of `accepted`:
   "I built it to be heavy... the last thing I told it"). This spends his
   best reveal in the first conversation, and "told it" implies a stone that
   follows orders, which contradicts the non-mystical premise. Keep him
   cagey until tea terms; replace "told it" with remembering a pose.
7. **The rewrite has Pip reveal Orrin's role** ("the miller's dad said Orrin
   built it"). Same problem. Keep the original Pip line ("not mean, just
   still doing its job"), which is better anyway.
8. **"Old ledger buried under the north bridge" is a fetch quest with a
   shovel.** Mara has the ledger and always has. The mystery is a page she
   can't read and is afraid of, which is a person, not a dig site.
9. **Stock character secrets.** Pip "afraid of the dark despite bragging",
   Orrin's "hands starting to shake", Mara "fears history will repeat",
   Elara "fascinated by danger". Each is a trope that could belong to any
   village. I've swapped each for a secret that's tied to *this* history
   (Hazel's fear, the signpost, the unreadable page, being stranded).
10. **Elara's blue moss "forget your own name for an hour"** is fun, but
    it's the only magic in the draft that acts on minds, and it pulls the
    tone toward potion-comedy. Turncaps and keeper's moss do lore work as
    well as being charming.
11. **Contradiction in the existing text: "the carters mentioned" the
    traveler, and Mara waits "when the carters come through"**, but the road
    has been closed thirty years. Fix: the **Low Road**, the slow western
    valley road, still carries carters. The Lantern Road was the fast eastern
    route. It also gives Mara's parents somewhere to have gone.
12. **Out-of-scope flag: `emberHint` in `world.ts` names Habitica and XP
    inside the hearth's in-world dialogue.** That's the one place the
    fourth-wall rule currently breaks. It should be a UI hint outside the
    dialogue box, or in-voice ("Embers come to you from the work you do
    elsewhere. Gather them at the hearth.").
