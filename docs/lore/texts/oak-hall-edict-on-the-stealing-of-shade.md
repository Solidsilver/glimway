# The Oak Hall Edict on the Stealing of Shade

**Author:** The Magistrates of the Oak Hall, Tarrow-on-the-Reed
**Date:** Chartered Year 122
**Material:** Vellum broadside, stamped with the wax Wheel and Wave. Posted at the Count Post.

**TO ALL WOODCUTTERS, CARTERS, AND FORAGERS OF THE EASTERN REACHES:**

Let it be known that the Iron-Oak ( *Quercus ferrum* ) of the Brackenwood and outer Wilds is the lifeblood of the Reed river trade, the foundation of our barges, and the sole property of the future. 

It takes two lifetimes of men for a single Iron-Oak to mature its rings into the density required for a true barge-knee. Therefore, the cutting of any standing Iron-Oak is strictly prohibited unless said tree has been inspected, measured, and struck with the lead-and-wax seal of the Oak Hall (The Wheel and the Wave) upon its trunk.

**Article IV: On the Stealing of Shade**
Any person found felling an unmarked tree shall be guilty of *Stealing Shade*. They have robbed not the current magistrate, but the grandchildren of this valley who would have sat beneath its boughs or sailed upon its timber. 

**The Penalty:**
The fine for Stealing Shade is fixed at the Hall Price: Fifty silver weights, or three drops of storm-grade amber, payable immediately at the Count House. Failure to pay will result in the forfeiture of the offender's cart, oxen, and Carters' Compact charter.

*Addendum by custom:* Windfall may be scavenged freely, provided the gatherer leaves a hand of leaves (three leafy branches) at the stump, so the wardens know the fall was the work of the wind, and not the saw.

*(In the margin of the copy kept at Ashwatch, in a Keeper's hand:)*

Three drops of storm-grade, for a fine. The Hall has never kept one lamp burning through a single Quiet, and never will. They set prices on what they have never had to carry. *Noted.* — W.H.

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts of Hearthwick and the Lantern Road"*
