# Dorrit's Second Span

**Author:** Dorrit Venn, Apprentice
**Date:** Thirty-two years ago (the summer before the storms).
**Material:** Chalked bridge sketch on a heavy iron-oak plank, found half-buried at the Tangle crossing.

*Master Orrin,*

*I drew the second span out like you asked. I know you said to use the iron brackets on the lower joint, but I've been watching the dirt here on the east bank. It doesn't settle; it breathes.*

*If we bracket the lower joint tight, and the bank breathes out, the tension is going to tear the kingpost right out of its socket. It's the weakest point. It's too rigid.*

*I keep thinking about the question I chalked on your shed door. If the hill wants to wander, do we step with it, or do we remind it to stay put? I think we should use oak trunnels on the lower joint, the way you peg the bench. Let the joint groan. Let it flex when the hill steps. If we make it too stubborn, the storm will just break it in half.*

*I'll cut the trunnels in the morning before the next train comes through. If I'm wrong, you can deduct the oak from my wages.*

*— D.V.*

*From "The Builders and the Breakers: Tradecraft of the Reaches"*
