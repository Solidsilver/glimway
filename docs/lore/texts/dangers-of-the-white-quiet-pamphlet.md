# "On the Dangers of the White Quiet" (Survival Pamphlet)

**Author:** The Tarrow Magistrates (Drafted by the Oak Hall Wardens)
**Date:** Issued annually every Smoke-wick.
**Material:** Cheap broadside paper, usually nailed to tavern doors and toll posts.

**WARNING TO ALL WINTER TRAVELERS:**
The season of the White Quiet is approaching. While the Fast Drift slows, the deep cold presents a unique geographical hazard. 

As the ground freezes, the Wilds enter a state of deep preservation. The land will attempt to "keep" whatever rests upon it for too long. 

*   **NEVER sleep directly on the ground.** If you camp in the outer Wilds during the Quiet, you must sleep in a cart bed, or elevate a canvas cot on stone blocks. Travelers who fall asleep on the bare earth will find their cloaks, hair, and eventually their skin frozen inextricably into the topsoil. The land will claim you as a memory.
*   **Frostbite:** In the Reaches, frostbite does not begin with pain; it begins with numbness, followed by a sensation of *absence*. Carters have reported that it feels as though they have simply forgotten they have toes. 
*   **Goods and Gear:** The Quiet does not distinguish a sleeping man from his sleeping goods. An axe left against a stump, a sledge left in the traces, a dropped mitten — all of it will be "kept" by morning, sunk into the crust as though it had grown there. Do NOT dig for it; tearing the crust draws the frost's attention to your hands. Mark the spot with a stone, keep moving, and come back after the turn. The land gives back what it has finished remembering.
*   **Thawing:** If you find a frozen limb, do not plunge it into hot water. The sudden return of sensation will shock the heart. Thaw the limb slowly against the belly of a living person or ox, and speak to the patient continuously to keep them anchored.

The Wardens add this, and ask that it be read aloud at every toll post through the Quiet: a cold man argues, but a frozen man agrees with anything. Sleep high, carry light, and keep talking.

*From "Blood, Sap, and Memory: Medical and Survival Texts of the Reaches"*
