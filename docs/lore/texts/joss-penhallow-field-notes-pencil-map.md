# Joss Penhallow’s Field Notes (The "Pencil Map" Progenitor)

**Author:** Joss Penhallow (Runner of the Lost Expedition)
**Date:** Written over a period of fifteen years, from his desk in Sallow Ford.
**Material:** Scraps of vellum, sewn together with heavy thread.

*Note:* You cannot draw a line. A line is a promise the land will not keep.

If you must map the western route [back to Hearthwick], you must stop thinking of the land as a place, and start thinking of it as a schedule.

*Rule 1: Map the Anchor, not the Shore.*
Only mark the places where a light has burned. Old iron-oak stumps, ruined shrines, the Ashwatch stones. These are the "bones." The bones do not move, because they are heavy with memory.

*Rule 2: Map the Tendency.*
Between the bones, the flesh of the woods wanders. The Tangle does not have a set path through it. Instead, record what it *wants* to do.
- *The deep ravines tend to fold shut during the Green Hush.*
- *The ridges flatten out during the White Quiet.*

*Rule 3: Use Pencil.*
Never commit ink to the space between the bones. Ink implies permanence. The moment you ink a river crossing in the deep Wilds, you guarantee you will drown there next season when the water moves.

*Rule 4: Trust the Turncaps.*
A turncap spins with the wind all its life, but give it twenty winters and it settles tilted, a hair off true, toward the nearest light that ever held steady. Wood remembers what it turned to face. If the bones have lost you, find a roof — sheep-shed, smoke-house, shrine — and mark which way its cap leans. It is pointing at a light that held. Set your camp where it points.

*(Elara Quill later found a copy of this specific page in a Tarrow market. She wrote "FINALLY, SOMEONE WITH EYES" in the margins).*

*(A later scrap, sewn last, the hand less steady than the first:)*
Fifteen years of pencil, so here is the last rule free of charge. The land forgets, and that is its mercy. It does not grieve. That work falls to us.
Nan read route marks the way other folk read faces. Three winters gone now, and I still turn every mark to the light, waiting for it to be told what it is feeling. Hollis lights the big lamp at the Ford every dusk and has not missed one yet. Tam talks to the oxen more than to us. We do not go back. A road is a line, and a line is a promise the land will not keep.
Pencil everything. Erase nothing.

*(Beneath this, in ink, in a stranger's hand: "THE ONE LINE WORTH INKING. — E.Q.")*

*From "The Liar's Art: Cartography and Exploration in the Reaches"*
