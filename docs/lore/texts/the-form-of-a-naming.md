# The Form of a Naming

**Author:** Keeper Wenna Hale, copied out by Mara Hale
**Date:** Before the Closure. The copy is new; the ink is still tacky.
**Material:** Two ledger leaves in Mara's hand, from the middle pages of the Ashwatch ledger.

A lamp is named so a stranger can find their way by it. Not after anyone. Not for anyone. A name nobody can read in the dark is no use.

Say where it stands in the line. Say what it stands by: something real, that a walker can see from the stone. Say what is behind them and what is ahead. Then close it.

*You are the first lamp past the Whitequiet. Behind you the road; ahead the old shrine. Hold fast.*

That is the first stone north of the quiet, and the first I cut. Use the same order every time, so a tired carter can say it without thinking.

Closings, three only: *Keep the weave tight.* *Hold fast.* *Stay.* Nan wants a fourth. Nan may cut her own stones.

Cut it once. A lamp that goes dark keeps its name. Whoever lights it again reads it, and does not cut over it.

*Under it, in Mara's hand:* She wrote "Noted" in the margin every time a lamp was named. I've started doing the same. — M.H.

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts of Hearthwick and the Lantern Road"*
