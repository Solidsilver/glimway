# The Boy Who Ran Faster Than the Wick

*A classic cautionary tale told to Hearthwick children who are too impatient to wait for the adults to finish their work. Often told while walking home at dusk.*

There was once a boy named Elian who lived at the bottom of the hill, and his best friend lived at the top. Every evening, Elian’s mother would say, "Wait for the lamplighter, Elian. The path does not exist until the Keeper says it does."

But Elian had a new wooden hoop and a stick, and he did not want to wait. The sun was dipping below the Tangle, and the shadows were getting long, but Elian thought, *I know the way. It’s just thirty steps up the hill.*

He started running. He ran past the first lamp-post, which was cold and dark. He took ten steps. He took twenty steps.

But the woods were having a restless night, and the land had not been reminded of the hill all day. By his twenty-fifth step, Elian looked down, and the dirt path was gone. He was standing on soft, gray moss. He looked back, but the bottom of the hill had wandered away. He looked up, but his friend's house had stepped aside to let a grove of sallow-trees pass through. 

Elian was standing in the Nowhere-Between.

He started to cry, but crying does not anchor the ground. He started to run, but running in the unanchored dark only tangles the woods faster. Soon, roots were growing over his shoes, trying to forget him.

Then, he heard a sound. *Scuff, step. Scuff, step.* A golden light pierced the moss. It was the Keeper, muttering the names of the stones. The light hit Elian, and instantly, the ground remembered it was a hill. The roots snapped back into the soil. 

The Keeper picked Elian up by the scruff of his neck. "The road is a promise," the Keeper scolded, tapping Elian's nose with a soot-stained thumb. "If you run ahead of the promise, the ground has no reason to catch you."

And Elian never walked past a dark glass again.

*(Parents do not quite end it there. They tap the window-lamp and ask, "And what did the Keeper say to the light?" And the child must answer before sleep: "Behind you the village, ahead of you the dark. Keep the promise." For that is how the ground is held—by telling every little light exactly where it stands. Watching buys you an hour, but a lamp that knows its place buys you a road.)*

*From "Bedtime Stories and Cautionary Tales of the Reaches"*
