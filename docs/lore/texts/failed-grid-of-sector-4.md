# The Failed Grid of Sector 4 (Surveyor’s Log)

**Author:** Initiate Surveyor Finch, Sallow Ford Institute of Cartography
**Date:** Twenty-two years ago.
**Material:** A water-damaged logbook. The first pages are immaculately ruled in grid-lines; the final pages are frantic, chaotic sketches.

**Day 1 (Bloom-wick, 4th):**
Established baseline observation post on the eastern ridge of Sector 4. Triangulated the distance between three prominent waymarks: a bifurcated ash tree, a limestone outcropping, and the river bend. The geometry is sound. Sector 4 is stable.

**Day 3 (Bloom-wick, 6th):**
Visibility was poor yesterday. Upon resuming observations today, the angles no longer sum to 180 degrees. The bifurcated ash tree is now forty yards closer to the river bend. I suspected a calibration error with my brass transit. Spent the afternoon stripping and cleaning the instrument.

**Day 5 (Bloom-wick, 8th):**
The transit is fine. The math is fine. The ground is a liar.
The limestone outcropping has completely vanished, replaced by a sunken hollow filled with nettles. The river has not merely moved; it has politely stepped aside to allow a grove of hazel trees to pass through. I attempted to draw a new grid, but watching the ash tree for three hours gave me intense vertigo. It is moving while I look at it, like the minute hand of a clock.

**Day 7 (Bloom-wick, 10th):**
I am submitting my resignation to the Institute. You cannot draw a grid over a living, breathing animal, and you cannot map the Wilds. I am mapping dirt that does not wish to be remembered. I recommend the Institute invest in poets rather than surveyors; they are better equipped for this nonsense.

**Day 9 (Bloom-wick, 12th):**
Watching buys you an hour. I have tested the saying to its roots. It is true, and it is not enough. I kept watch on the hollow from dusk until the middle of the night and the nettles did not so much as tremble. Then I slept, and by morning the hollow had an inlet in it, and the inlet had a heron standing in that, patient as a debt. The heron was not there when the land agreed with my map, and I do not think the land has noticed the difference.
I have torn out the ruled pages. Let the Institute keep its grids; I am keeping the sketches, which have at least stayed honest. Going home by the long shore. Where I find a stump with rings tight as a snail's shell, I will make my camp beside it, because something held that ground once, and the ground does not forget everything.

*(Filed with the log: a stiff card, the Institute's seal gone to fuzz with damp.)*

RE: The resignation of Initiate Surveyor Finch. The Institute has considered it.
Resignation: declined. Report: filed under Weather.
Sector 4 was re-walked this Mud-wick and found stable. The ground, having drifted, has drifted back. This is not to the initiate's credit, but neither is it to the ground's.
The Institute reminds all initiates that held ground forces the forgetting to its edges — see the Weir Effect, first lecture, first winter. The Ford's lamp-ring holds the town. It cannot hold the ridge. Survey the held ground, initiate, and let the edges write their own poetry.
— The Registrar

*From "The Liar's Art: Cartography and Exploration in the Reaches"*
