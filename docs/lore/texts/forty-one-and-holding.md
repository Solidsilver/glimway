# Forty-One and Holding

**Author:** Finn Tolley
**Date:** Spanning the two winters since Aldo died.
**Material:** A tally scratched in clusters of five into the wooden side of the mill's hopper.

*Forty-one.*

That's how many times the wheel turns while the Wend carries a stick from the upper bend down to the grate. Dad timed it the day before he died, two winters ago.

He made me promise to clean the grate before the frost. He handed me the linseed box, heavy and smelling of old oil, and he told me to count the turns of the river and think about how long it takes a piece of pine to float forty-one wandering turns in the dark.

He kept a scratch on this hopper for every fox he cleaned off the grate, and I've kept it up since he passed. Twenty-seven of the scratches are his. Twenty-seven carved foxes in the box.

I don't open the box anymore. I just run my thumb over the lid. The box was meant for Wenna, and Wenna is gone. If I take it to Mara Hale, she'll know. And if she knows, the whole village will know that the river is bringing things back from the dark.

I'm forty-one turns from the dark, and I'm holding. I'm just holding.

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts, Vol. II"*
