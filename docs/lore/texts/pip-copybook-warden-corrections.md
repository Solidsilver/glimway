# A Page from Pip’s Copybook

**Author:** Traditional / Pip Penhallow
**Date:** Last Mudrise
**Material:** Cheap paper, written in a child's sprawling, energetic pencil.

**The Skipping Rhyme (What the village says):**
Stone-still, stone-still, 
Who goes over Ashwatch hill?
Bring a rubbing or bring a light,
Stone-still lets you through tonight!

**My Scientific Corrections (by Pip Penhallow, Runner):**
1. The warden is NOT "stone-still." It moves at a rate of approximately one hand-span per unobserved hour, depending on the severity of the drift tide. (Source: Elara's tables and my own staring).
2. It does not "let you through." It is a jointed iron-oak frame built by Orrin. It simply reverts to a resting posture when the amber latch is engaged. 
3. Skipping to this song is historically inaccurate. You should walk at a steady, cautious pace because the ground is probably not where it was yesterday. 
4. "Who goes over Ashwatch hill?" is the wrong question. The hill itself has moved four hand-spans since Mudrise. (Source: the Pencil Map, revision six, and the fence post by the well, which does not wander. Elara checked my figures and only sighed once.)

*P.S. I asked Orrin if I could grease the warden's knee hinges so it wouldn't creak if it moved. He looked like he wanted to throw me in the well.*

*P.P.S. My whistle is still perfectly round. Uncle Joss dented his to one note you could hear clear across the square, which is the correct amount of dent. I am not going to dent mine until the right note finds me. Elara says you cannot hurry a note any more than you can hurry slack water, and she is usually right about moving things.*

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts, Vol. II"*
