# Orrin's Architectural Standard for "Drift-Slap" Foundations

**Author:** Master Builder Orrin
**Date:** Sometime in the last twenty years.
**Material:** A piece of heavy drafting vellum, nailed to the wall of the Hearthwick Builder's Shed. It is covered in aggressive charcoal diagrams.

**TO ALL APPRENTICES:**
If you lay a rigid, deep-trench stone foundation in Hearthwick, I will personally throw you in the Wend. 

Stone foundations are for Tarrow men who live on dead dirt. The ground here breathes. If you pin a house deep into the bedrock, and the topsoil experiences a slow drift, the earth will shear the house off at the ankles. We call this "Drift-Slap." It sounds like a cannon firing, and it drops the roof on the kitchen.

**The Hearthwick Method:**
1. You do not dig down. You level the topsoil.
2. You lay four massive skids of unseasoned iron-oak directly on the dirt. 
3. You build the floor joists atop the skids, using wooden pegs, not iron nails. Nails rust; pegs swell and hold.
4. The house must sit *on* the land like a barge sits on the river. If the hill wants to step left in the night, your house must be polite and step with it. 
5. Measure in fingers, not in spans. A span is a Count House measurement. Wood grows to nobody's inch, and the ground under it grows to less.

Your anchor-light will keep the house from wandering into the Tangle, but the house must have the flexibility to shimmy in its own footprint. If the floorboards don't creak in the autumn, you built it too tight.

*Chalked on the doorframe of the shed itself, low down where the vellum doesn't reach, in a quick slanted hand gone grey with years: "And if the hill forgets where it was going — do we step with it, or remind it? — D.V."*

*(The chalk is older than the vellum nailed above it. It has outlasted two re-nailings and every scrubbing it was ever given, of which there have been none in some years.)*
