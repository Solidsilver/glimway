# The Ashwatch Skipping Game

*A playground rhyme used by the children of Hearthwick. They draw a grid in the dirt and jump between the squares, pretending the lines are the Stone Warden trying to catch them.*

**Leader:** 
Stone-still, stone-still!
Who goes over Ashwatch hill?

**Jumpers (hopping on one foot):**
I go over, I go through!
I have a rubbing in my shoe!

**Leader (turning their back and covering their eyes):**
The lantern's cold, the latch is tight!
The stone is walking in the night!

*(The Jumpers must freeze perfectly still. The Leader spins around quickly.)*

**Leader:**
I see a twitch, I see a shake!
Two weaves and a heavy break!

*(If the Leader catches a Jumper moving, that child is "caught by the Warden" and must sit out. If a Jumper makes it across the grid while the Leader's back is turned, they tap the Leader's shoulder and yell "LIGHT!" to win.)*

**Round two — the Warden's Walk:**

**Leader (walking the grid, heel to toe, very slowly):**
Stone walks slow when no one sees!
Stone walks back where the Warden please!

**Jumpers (creeping with arms held straight out, exactly as slow as the Leader):**
Warden, Warden, do not frown,
Show the rubbing and sit down!

*(In this round nobody runs. A Jumper who laughs, hurries, or drops their arms is "walking free of the hill" and sits out. A Jumper who reaches the Leader holds up the sole of their shoe—the rubbing—and calls "TWO WEAVES AND A BREAK!" The Leader must groan, sit down where they stand, and be a stone until the next round begins. No Jumper may cross while the Leader's eyes are shut twice in a row; even the stone only walks so far from its post.)*

*From "Bedtime Stories and Cautionary Tales of the Reaches"*
