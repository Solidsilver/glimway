# Scraps from Orrin’s Workshop

**Author:** Master Builder Orrin
**Material:** Slabs of pale pine, nailed roughly to the lintel of his workshop door. Written in thick, angry charcoal.

**RULES FOR THE BENCH (Read them or go home):**

1.  **Wood breathes. Drift-wood wanders.** If you build a rigid, perfectly square box out of common pine in the Reaches, the land will rack it to kindling in a week. Buildings must be allowed to flex. If it doesn't creak, it's going to snap.
2.  **Measure in fingers, not in numbers.** Numbers are from Tarrow. Numbers lie when the humidity changes. Your fingers belong to you.
3.  **The Hall Price of hinges is a robbery.** Therefore, we use wooden trunnels (pegs) wherever possible. Oak pegs in pine holes. The pine shrinks, the oak holds, the joint lives. If I catch you using a Tarrow iron nail on a non-load-bearing joist, I will deduct it from your wages.
4.  **Drift-stone is for foundations, NOT for walls.** You can use unanchored stone to line a well or pack a post-hole, because it has nowhere to wander. If you build a chimney out of drift-stone, it will step into the living room while the family sleeps.
5.  **Clean your tools.** Rust is just iron forgetting it is a saw.

*Note appended at the bottom, carved deep into the wood rather than written in charcoal:*
**6. Never stand downstream of a tension chain.**

*Added below, in charcoal, the letters pressed harder into the pine:*
**7. The boxwood folding rule on the nail by the door is not common property.** Boxwood barely drinks the damp. It holds its length through weather that has everything else wandering, which is the whole trick of building in the Reaches and most of the trick of living in it. Fingers for feeling; the rule for checking the fingers. And the D.V. scratched on the back is none of your business.

*Beneath Rule 7, in chalk, in a small, careful hand — the letters leaning a little crooked:*
*"Six trunnels cut for the lintel today. All six fit. — D.V."*

*From "The Builders and the Breakers: Tradecraft of the Reaches"*
