# Annotated Page from "Flora of the Eastern Reaches"

**Author:** Scholar unknown. Annotations by Elara Quill.
**Date:** Printed 80 years ago. Annotations made last summer.
**Material:** Printed book page, heavily marked with aggressive red ink.

**Printed Text:**
*...The mysterious Keeper’s Moss (Bryum Anchoris) is a magical warding lichen found exclusively along the Lantern Road. It is widely believed by the rustic populations that the moss feeds upon the bravery of the Carters, growing only where men of stout heart have stood firm against the darkness...*

**Red Ink Marginalia (Elara):**
*Absolute rubbish. The moss feeds on the trace mineral residue of burnt storm-grade amber. It is a biological byproduct of anchor-light, not a measure of "stout hearts." It grows on the route stones because the stones are saturated with oil soot. The author of this book has clearly never been east of the Sallow ferry.*

**Printed Text:**
*...Furthermore, the deep Wilds are governed by malevolent spirits that rearrange the paths to trap the unwary. Only the holy light of the lanterns holds them at bay...*

**Red Ink Marginalia:**
*It is called Drift. It has a tide. It has slack water. It has a flood. If these Hearthwick people would stop trying to nail the tablecloth to the floor and just learned to read a tide chart, they wouldn't need to burn their weight in amber every winter. I am crossing this entire paragraph out. Do not let Pip read this book.*

**Printed Text:**
*...Certain marsh-dwelling peoples are said to "ride the wandering" of the land itself, timing their journeys to the earth's movement as a sailor times a tide. No credence ought be given such tales in a serious botanical work...*

**Red Ink Marginalia:**
*Nine leagues on a full flood, if the poling-staff is shod right and you can taste the wind coming off the mud before you see it. In the Merrow Saltings we keep drift-tables—slack water, neap, flood—and the land arrives when it is properly asked, not a moment before. A table can be copied by anyone with a stylus. The trick is knowing what the water remembers. (I have said too much to a book. There is no Saltings. I have merely read.)*

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts, Vol. II"*
