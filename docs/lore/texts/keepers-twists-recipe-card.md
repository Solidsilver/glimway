# Recipe Card: Keeper’s Twists

**Author:** Hazel Penhallow
**Date:** Ink is old, but grease stains are recent.
**Material:** A flour-dusted index card, pinned to the bakery wall.

**Keeper's Twists**
*(Makes one dozen, if the runner doesn't steal the dough)*

- 3 cups milled flour (Ask Finn for the fine sift, not the grist)
- 1 cup warm water
- A pinch of Tarrow salt
- 1 heavy spoon of honey
- *Crossed out: 2 spoons Sallow Ford pressing oil*
- *Added in pencil:* 2 spoons churned butter. It makes them heavy, but what else are we to do? 

Knead until it fights back. Roll into long strips, fold in half, and twist the two ends together. The twist is the important part—it has to look like a rope, or the Carters won't buy it. Glaze with egg wash. Bake until golden. 

*Note on the back:* 
Joss liked the ends burnt. He’d sit on the flour sack and play that blasted dented whistle until I gave him the burnt ones just to buy some quiet. I made a batch with butter today. They don't taste right. The crumb is too short. I threw them out for the birds. I won't make them again until the oil comes back. 

*Second note, in fresher ink:* 
Carting Day again. They hung the hame on the Commons gate, polished up bright for a horse that isn't coming. I set one Twist on the gate beside it, burnt end and all. Told the market women it was for luck, which is a lie. It's for late. When the oil comes back and the long way home brings him through that gate, he can start there.

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts, Vol. II"*
