# Field Notes: E. Quill

**Author:** Elara Quill
**Date:** Summer (The Green Hush), current year.
**Material:** Waterproofed parchment notebook, neat, cramped handwriting. 

*Entry 41: Observations on Clitocybe vertens (Local name: Turncaps)*

I am continually astounded by the Hearthwick approach to the drift. They view it as an adversary to be wrestled into submission. They spend precious amber trying to nail the tablecloth down, rather than simply learning when the table is going to be set. 

Today's transect in the eastern Tangle yielded fascinating data. *C. vertens* is a fruiting fungi that exhibits phototropism, but not to the sun. The caps invariably tilt toward the nearest active anchor-light. It is a biological compass pointing to held memory. 

In the inner woods, they tilt west, toward the village square and Ada's window. But past the third old route-stone... the alignment shifts. 

I took three measurements today. A ring of turncaps on a dead stump, a cluster by a dried creek bed, and a single large specimen beneath a hazel stand. 

They are tilting East. 

There is no anchor-light east of here. Not according to Mara’s ledger. Not according to the thirty-year history of this stubborn, grieving town. 

But the fungi do not lie. The drift has slack water over that ridge. Someone out there has been keeping a light. I must calibrate my tables. I will not tell the miller. He will only start counting again.

*Addendum, past midnight:* A fourth reading, taken at the old route-stone where the village maps give out. Seven caps on one dead birch, all bearing east and a hair to the north, every one in agreement. My Salting tables chart the pull of every light I have ever fed them. There is no column for a light I have never seen.

*(Tucked between these pages, a strip torn from ledger paper, in a rounder, more careful hand:)*

E. — Whatever tilts east of the third stone, it tilts without oil from my stores. The ledger says the chain is dark, and the ledger is not sentimental. If someone is keeping a light out there, they are either lost or they are not ours. Stay west of it. And if you are right — then my grandmother's last page is not what the village believes it is. I am not ready to read it. *Noted.* — M.H.

*From "Voices of the Drift: Found Texts of Hearthwick and the Lantern Road"*
