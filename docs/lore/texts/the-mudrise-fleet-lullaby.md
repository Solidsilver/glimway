# The Mudrise Fleet (A Lullaby)

*Sung by parents during the Mudrise season, specifically on the eve of 'The Breaking' festival, when children prepare their walnut-shell boats.*

Crack the shell and scoop the meat,
Here comes the Mudrise fleet.
A hazel leaf to catch the wind,
A drop of tallow tucked within.

The ice goes out, the river wakes,
It shivers off the winter aches.
So put your boat upon the Wend,
And wave goodbye to your little friend.

Where do the candle-hulls go to sleep?
Down where the water is dark and deep.
They carry the frost and the dark away,
To buy us a long and a sunlit day.

Whisper low to the walnut shell,
"Dark water, keep my lantern well:
It is a boat, its sail is set—
Remember it, and don't forget."

If your little ship sails the farthest of all,
The oak leaves will hold until late in the fall.
So sleep now, my darling, the river is cold,
But your tiny ship is brave and bold.

*From "Bedtime Stories and Cautionary Tales of the Reaches"*
