# Lore pass: working notes

The owner's calls during the lore pass (exp/lore-final, from 2026-10-19), in the order they came.
These feed `canon.md`. When canon.md is final it supersedes this file.

Sources: `chronicle.md` (the canon so far), `drafts/glimway-lore-magic-bible.md` (the owner's new
draft), the found texts in `texts/`, and the shipped game.

## Settled

1. **The Drift is a wound, not a villain.** The forgetting was caused by someone the Dark had
   infected, long ago. The forgetting itself is not evil: the world is hurting. **The Dark is
   evil**, aware, and separate from the Drift. A biome may show the corruption plainly.
2. **People in town get personal stakes in the Dark's victims.** Elara's mother at Sallow Ford was
   only an example; it is not canon. Elara stays from the Merrow Saltings; Sallow Ford stays the
   place the four survivors reached and where Hollis keeps his lamp.
3. **The Company of the Road merges into the Carters' Compact** (its last generation). Its
   fracturing is the Weir Years, the Winter of Two Storms and the lean years after.
4. **A wick is seven days and the Turning comes every wick.** Time is compressed and the lore
   keeps the length of a year vague; the changing world makes the days hard to count.
5. **Care is strong, and oil and lamps make it stronger.** The lore must not promise that care
   alone holds land until a mechanic does. Idea, not decided: more visits or time spent at a
   place could slow its turning.
6. **New systems are canon as "later".** Practices, ranks, cross-workings, delves, bosses, boss
   branches and Bleached Hollows are recorded but not applied. No player is above level 8 yet.
7. **Hollis's lamp answers the Dark.** The Dark says nobody is coming; Hollis kept a light for a
   road nobody could walk, for thirty years.
8. **Pell's crate is old magic**: the drift gives back what was made with care. The crate was
   made long ago by a powerful, good person.
9. **The Dark is old.** It has worked through the world's history long before the Two Storms;
   Silas's fear at the fork is one small instance, not the start.
10. **The Stone Warden is the honest opposite of a binding**: it was named, not ordered, and its
    naming can be turned round. Later: attacking the Warden makes things worse.
11. **The Dark's temptation gets a plainer name** ("the Short Way", "the Easy Way" or similar),
    freeing "the Old Ways" for the paired-lamp travel working.
12. **The player's Unmoored sway is the Dark's first touch**, shared with Dark-fevered beasts.
13. **Hesper is bound in the brackets at Dorrit's Span (0.15).** Hesper, the Keeper before Wenna,
    refused the closure and cut a Binding Rune at the Tangle crossing so the road could never be
    closed; her binding lives in Orrin's iron brackets. The fight is with the span; the
    redemption is Hesper's, by unwinding her naming the way the Warden is settled; the rebuilt
    span is Dorrit's flexing design.
14. **The Weir Effect had both causes.** The road was over-pinned out of ordinary greed (Hemlock's
    text stands); the Dark worked that crack; Hesper's binding at the crossing turned the second
    storm into a stir.
15. **The temptation is "the Short Way"** (those who take it: short-wayers).
16. **Pell's crate is a family heirloom** (superseded the "maker unknown" call; see 25).

17. **Fallbrine** did the deep-history deed. No epithet; the story reveals what he did.
18. **No single name for the fallen.** Different people call them different things: some are
    called "lost", some "obsessed". Overwork and over-forcing lead to the Dark too, not only
    idleness and shortcuts.
19. **Corrupted pockets are "frets"** ("the fret on Marrow Fen").
20. **The Dark** in speech; **the Draught** in Keepers' writing (proposed; the owner didn't object).

21. **Fallbrine's deed is "the Unyoking"** in history; Fallbrine himself called it "the Release".
    The Dark gets in where care stops, by doing too little or forcing too much.

22. **Road geography.** Sallow Ford lies north-east. The old Lantern Road left by the East Gate,
    climbed past Ashwatch to the hilltop shrine, then swung north through the Tangle crossing
    (Dorrit's Span) and on through the Whitequiet. The shrine's "east of here" line becomes north.
23. **Turncaps** (owner asked for the explanation; the game already matches): grey mushrooms
    whose caps lean toward the nearest held light. A homing turncap is one set in amber while it
    still leans toward town.
24. **The Practices replace magic.md's four class-locked crafts (later).** The Habitica class is
    a lean: a head start in one Practice (Warrior: Handcraft, Mage: Songcraft, Healer:
    Hearthcraft, Rogue: Waycraft).
25. **Pell's crate came down Pell's family.** Pell's father gave it to him; it has passed down
    for generations, maybe made by an ancestor, maybe given to one. The Pells have worked to
    understand the land (hence deeds) and the way it gives things back. Sometimes things go
    missing (to settle: never a player's own things).

26. **Canon draft 1 written** (`canon.md`). Confirmed from it: Hesper's bound joint is why the
    bridge broke, so she is partly responsible for Bett's and Dorrit's deaths; every lamp that
    holds land has a naming (a lot lantern's is its deed, a way-lamp's is spoken as it's set, a
    communal lamp is held by footsteps and hollow until named); only the old world's things go
    missing from Pell's crate, never a player's; "the drift" is lower case.
