# Glimway canon

**Status: final (2026-10-19), settled with the owner.** This is the single
source of truth for Glimway's lore. It supersedes `chronicle.md`, everything under `drafts/`
(the world bibles, the rewrites, the three takes and the owner's magic bible) and the lore
passages in design docs. The found texts in `texts/` stay as primary sources: where one
disagrees with this file, this file wins and the text is corrected.

How to read the tags:

- **[shipped]**: in the game today (0.13). Text that describes it must match how it works.
- **[later]**: canon, but not built. Players may hear of it in stories and texts. No dialogue,
  item or help text may promise it as something they can do now.

Section 12 lists terms and their one spelling. Section 14 lists what was settled at review and what is parked. `apply-map.md` says where this lands in the game.

## 1. The world in one page

The world remembers itself badly. Long ago a maker called **Fallbrine**, listening to the Dark,
tried to free people from work for ever: he cut binding runes into the bedrock so the earth
would give harvest, light and permanence with no one's care. History calls it **the Unyoking**.
It cracked the world's foundation. Since then everything above the bedrock forgets: every wick
(seven days), whatever is not walked, lit or held **turns**, slipping back into what might have
been. The forgetting is a wound, not a villain. **The Dark** came in through the crack, and the
Dark is evil.

People answer the forgetting with care, made stronger by light. A lamp with oil and a
**naming** holds the land around it. Paths walked by friends stay paths. Things made with care
come back when the land turns. Light gathers in **glims**: amber beads holding light kindled
by kept promises.

**Hearthwick** is an old company town at the edge of the Wilds, built around the **Wick Tree**.
It once sent iron-oak and amber along the **Lantern Road**, a chain of named lamps kept by the
village's Keepers. Thirty-one years ago, in the **Winter of Two Storms**, the road broke. Keeper
Wenna Hale closed it; six people went out under the closure mark anyway to fetch oil; two died
at the Tangle crossing; four reached **Sallow Ford** and never came home. Wenna learned they
lived and kept it secret, because the road could not be kept and the village would have died
looking for them.

The player arrives and the road becomes holdable again. Every lamp lit removes a little of the
reason for Wenna's silence. At the end of the road a light is waiting: Hollis Brack has kept a
lamp burning at Sallow Ford for thirty years, for a road nobody could walk. The Dark's one
argument is that nobody is coming and work is pointless. Hollis's lamp is the answer.

## 2. Cosmology

### 2.1 The Deep Measure [shipped as a rule]

The world's bedrock (ridges, lake basins, riverbeds) was laid down to an old, slow foundation
called **the Deep Measure**. Stone and deep water are heavy enough and old enough to remember
their own shape. They do not turn.

*In the game:* lasting geography. Lakes, rivers and ridges keep their place; living detail turns.
The Wend keeps its bed every wick; the land along it turns.

It was not always this calm. Before the Two Storms the drift ran all the time, and harder in the
Weir Years: rivers wandered across their valley floors, outcrops sank, and stirs folded ravines.
The old texts that say so are true for their time. Since the road broke and the pressure bled
off, the drift moves only at the Turning, and the Measure shows through.

### 2.2 The Unyoking and the Crack [later, as story]

Long ago, **Fallbrine**, a brilliant maker, came to resent the slow rhythm of work: ploughing,
mending, walking, tending lamps. The Dark whispered to him. He set out to free people from toil
for ever by cutting vast coercive **binding runes** into the Deep Measure, forcing the earth to
yield harvest, light and permanence by itself, with no one's care. He called it **the Release**.
Everyone since calls it **the Unyoking**.

You cannot harvest light without care. The runes cracked the Measure. Instead of everlasting
ease, the world's memory broke into the weekly Turning, and the Dark came in through **the
Crack**. Who Fallbrine was and what he did are revealed slowly, through story; most people only
know the word "Unyoking" and that it went wrong. *[later]:* the original cracked runes lie deep
in the earth; finding them and beginning to heal the world's memory is the far horizon of the
game.

### 2.3 The drift and the Turning [shipped]

**The drift** is the world's forgetting: "the land forgets what it isn't reminded of." It is not
evil and not angry. The world is hurting, not wicked.

- **The Turning.** Every wick (seven days) the drift's judgment falls. Wild land turns: its
  trees, undergrowth, generated trails and ruins slip back into what might have been, and come
  up different. What is held stays, and so does everything within the Wick Tree's reach (2.8).
  *In the game:* the weekly wick turning; the Keeper posts it on the notice board a day ahead
  ("Dark of Amber-wick — the outer Wilds will turn."). The Tangle never turns.
- **A stir.** In storms the drift runs fast, and unheld land moves in minutes. A stir grew a root
  through a cart axle in one night and tore the Tangle crossing in the Winter of Two Storms.
  Stirs are history and story; the game has no stirs [later, if ever].
- **Watching and walking.** Attention slows the drift; feet keep a way open; light holds it.
  The saying is "watching buys you an hour; light buys you a road."

The chronicle's "slow drift" (paths wandering a few feet a season) is history, not the present:
it ended with the Two Storms (2.1). Today, between turnings, land stays put. Seasons change how
the land looks and feels, not how fast it moves.

### 2.4 The Whitequiet [shipped as a place; the biome later]

*Draft, 2026-10-20, written from the owner's own words with the ideas partner. The owner edits.*

The pale, hushed country where the land has **stopped trying**. In the green Wilds the weekly
Turning is the hurting earth still trying: each wick it reaches again for its trees, streams and
seasons. In the Whitequiet it has given up that hard work. It forgets itself, and it forgets
those who walk into it, which is why people so often get lost there. It is a sad place: part of
the earth that has given up.

It is where the Unyoking's curse came nearest to winning. Fallbrine wanted a world with no need
for work (2.2), and the Whitequiet is that world: nothing is tended, nothing is mended, and
nothing asks to be. It lulls whoever enters toward a dreamy, sleepy end. The land is not angry;
it has been overcome. Waking any of it again takes more than a lamp: an old memory to start
from, stronger light, and living work brought in from the waking woods *[later]*.

What it hasn't forgotten stands out. The **trinkets** and **Echoes** that surface there (2.7)
are the last stubborn sparks of human care and unfinished work, warm against the pale grass,
because they still remember who they belong to.

The Whitequiet is one land among many, not the edge of the world. The world does have an edge,
very far off, where the land simply goes no further; it has no story.

*In the game:* today, the Whitequiet region north of the Tangle, past the Tangle crossing, which
turns every wick. *[later, 0.14]* a land of its own: thick pale fog, faint paths that lead
nowhere, losing your way once unmoored, hollows that hold Echoes, and no fights. Walking with a
friend keeps you both from losing your way; falling asleep there costs a few glims and sends the
outing's forage to Pell's crate. The world's border is "the world's edge" in player text. The
winter mark is called **Quiet**; "the White Quiet" as a name for winter or for the drift's
preserving state is retired in favour of the Whitequiet.

### 2.5 The Dark [later, mostly]

The Dark beyond the lamplight is not night. It is old, aware and patient, with one will: to snuff
every light, unmake memory, sever people from each other and drag every living thing into cold,
isolated silence. It came into the world through the Crack, and it uses the forgetting, but it is
not the forgetting.

- **How it works.** It presses on the edge of lamplight like cold water on glass, and it listens.
  It hates beauty, rhythm and kept promises, because they hold the world against it. It whispers
  to the tired, the proud, the bitter and the greedy, in familiar voices and old regrets: work is
  pointless, nobody is coming, the shortcut is wisdom.
- **It is old.** It has worked through every age since the Unyoking: in the Blind Routes'
  "toll of the dark", in every Keeper's warnings, in the Weir Years. Silas's fear at the
  Brackenwood fork is one small instance the village happens to know.
- **Names.** People say **the Dark**. Keepers write **the Draught**: the cold that comes through
  a crack and puts candles out. Scholars argue about whether it thinks at all; Elara Quill, for
  one, insists it is geology. The texts may disagree; the canon says it is real and aware.
- **Frets** [later]. Where the Dark festers unchecked, it drinks the colour, warmth and sound out
  of a valley or marsh: a **fret**. Frets can be pushed back with light, song and restoration.
  The corruption is plain to see there, unlike in the hurting but innocent Wild.
- **Unmoored** [shipped for the player, later for beasts]. The Dark's first touch. A hero who
  stays too long deep in the Tangle, where the Wick Tree's reach thins (2.8), who lingers in the
  Whitequiet without an anchor (2.4) *[later]*, or who is caught in a Turning, comes
  **unmoored**: the world sways and pales at the edges. It passes with rest on safe ground in
  town or at home, or at once with a remedy (a jar of turncaps; a salve eases it). Wilds lamps
  are not safe ground. Beasts the Dark has fevered are unmoored too (5.4).
- **Pressure and things of the Dark** [later]. The cold from the Crack doesn't press evenly.
  Across wide stretches of the Wilds, its **pressure** leans heavier where the earth is far from
  tended lamps and human footsteps, and the far reaches lie deeper still; a lit lamp calms the
  ground in its reach (the strongest light at a spot, never a stack of them). Where the Draught
  knots round a mockery of light, wind or shadow, it forms **things of the Dark** (5.4): wisps,
  fen-lanterns and cave shadows that were never alive and disperse when beaten.

### 2.6 Care, wear and the anchored [shipped as framing]

Care is strong, and light makes it stronger. A road stays a road because friends walk it. Care
wears in slowly: a new path needs feet every wick.

People who love their neighbours, take pride in honest craft, tend their hearths and make music
or art are **anchored**: the Dark finds them heavy and almost impossible to twist. The Dark gets
in where care stops, and care stops in two ways:

- **too little**: those who despise effort, hoard for themselves or sink into bitter isolation;
- **too much**: those who force work past care and will not let anything rest. Obsession opens
  the door as wide as idleness. Rest is part of care; that is why the road was meant to go dark
  in winter, and why Quiet is a season.

*[later idea, `ideas.md`]* A way walked several wicks running becomes **worn** and forgives
one missed wick. Not canon until built; the lore says only that care wears in slowly.

### 2.7 What the land gives back [shipped]

The drift does not destroy what was made with care and is owed to someone; it gives it back.

- **Trinkets.** The Whitequiet gives back things that were meant to come home: a dropped work
  glove, a birthday candle never lit, Tam's knotted ox-halter, eleven stamped road-nails.
- **Echoes.** Phantom camps of the Six: not ghosts, but the woods replaying a mundane, waiting
  moment. Lighting the **owed lamp** (with glims) lets the kettle boil and the tune finish, and
  the moment files itself away kindly.
- **What players made.** Everything a player made or placed comes back when its land turns.
  Nothing a player made is ever lost (the game's first rule). Where it comes back depends on the
  land (3.8): a camp's lamp, oil, pieces and plants (still growing) and unwalked paths go to
  Pell's crate; a lot's backland comes home, its pieces to home storage and its plants as seeds
  in the shared chest.

### 2.8 The Wick Tree's reach [shipped]

The Wick Tree's roots drink from the Deep Measure, and around the tree the land does not change:
it is held by the tree's reach. The tree is why a town could be built here at all; the company
came for the iron-oak and the amber, and stayed because of the tree. Everything inside the
reach stays as it is from wick to wick: Hearthwick, its lots and hearths, and the Tangle to the
north.

The Tangle is the scar of the stir that broke the road (4.3). The tree's reach holds it in its
torn shape: it never turns, whoever walks it and whatever lamps burn there. Deep in the Tangle,
toward the edge of the reach, the hold thins, and a traveller who lingers there comes unmoored
(2.5). Past the reach (the Whitequiet and the land beyond it) the land turns every wick.

*In the game:* town and lot ground are permanent; the Tangle is a still region (its `still`
epoch never ends); the Whitequiet and the generated Wilds turn at each wick.

## 3. Light and care: the rules people live by

### 3.1 Glims [shipped]

A **glim** is a bead of amber holding a glint of earned light. Light in this world has weight
and warmth, and it is kindled by care: keeping a promise, tending a duty, mending what is
broken, helping a neighbour. Kept promises leave light behind, and amber holds it. The Count
House cut glims to its measure, the Compact paid its lamplighters in them, and Hearthwick never
stopped. Glims don't fade. Raw amber is still sold by the drop; a glim is amber already cut and
lit.

*In the game:* glims come from the player's real kept promises (Habitica gold, topped up, at
most 30 a day). The game never names Habitica in the world; in-world, a glim is earned light.
Glims buy goods, settle Echoes, rest at the hearth and, *[later]*, feed workings.

### 3.2 The Wick Tree, sap and oil [shipped]

The **Wick Tree** grows at the heart of Hearthwick, and its roots drink from the Deep Measure.
Its golden sap, refined into **hearth oil**, doesn't only burn: it **witnesses**. While a lamp
burns, the land around it is seen, remembered and held in the present. The hearth-stone at its
foot keeps a lamp that is named and tended every dusk. The village is named for
the tree: a hearth at a wick. Around it, the land holds still (2.8).

- **Weekly sap.** Once a wick, anyone may tap the tree; the chandler refines the jar free into
  one flask of hearth oil, enough to keep one lot lantern for a wick. More oil is the chandler's,
  for glims.
- **Amber-oil** [history]. The road burned amber-oil, cut with tallow and pressed at Sallow
  Ford. It has not come up the road in thirty years; the village makes do with the tree.
- **Amber grades.** Sold by the drop, graded by how long it keeps a light after the flame is out:
  *candle-grade* (minutes), *hearth-grade* (an hour, for window lamps), *storm-grade* (all
  night; rare and dear).

### 3.3 Wicks: one word, three things [shipped]

- **A wick** (time): seven days, the stretch between one trimming of the lamps and the next.
  Twelve wicks make the year (3.10).
- **A wick of oil**: what a lamp burns in one wick. A lamp holds three.
- **A lamp wick**: the cord itself, an item. Relighting a dark lamp stone burns one.

### 3.4 The anchor-light: oil, naming, lamp [shipped]

A lamp holds land when three things meet:

1. **the oil**, the care that feeds it;
2. **the naming**, spoken to the flame: exactly where it stands and what it holds;
3. **the lamp**, the vessel: frame, glass, wick.

A lamp with oil but no naming burns **hollow**: it warms, it lights, it anchors nothing. A naming
is not a spell; it is telling the lamp what it is holding still. These three are the first and
commonest **working** (5.1): naming is its cadence, the lamp its vessel, the oil its care.

Every lamp that holds land has a naming, even when the player doesn't see one
spoken:

- a **lot lantern**'s naming is the lot's deed in Pell's ledger ("we do not deed the coordinates
  of the earth; we deed the anchor");
- a **way-lamp**'s setting is itself a short working that only a Keeper can do; that is why
  setting a camp needs the Keeper's hand, and why the lamp holds its chunk from the first night.
  Its **naming from parts** is its *lasting* name, the one strangers find their way by, given
  when its builders choose (or never): it goes in the ledger and leaves lamp ash. A camp holds
  land the same with or without it;
- a **communal lamp** is held by footsteps, not oil, and burns hollow until a Keeper names it.

### 3.5 Lamps in the world [shipped]

| Lamp | What it is | What keeps it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hearth lantern** | The lamp at the Wick Tree's foot | The village, every dusk |
| **Lot lantern** | On the stake behind a Hearthwick lot; holds the lot's backland | Oil (three wicks deep); anyone may fill it |
| **Way-lamp** | Set in the Wilds for a camp; holds one chunk | Oil, as a lot lantern |
| **Communal lamp** | A public rest light: a relit lamp stone or a post anyone sets | A visit each wick |
| **Lamp stone** | A dark lamp of the old Lantern Road, in stone; relit with a lamp wick | A visit each wick, once relit |
| **Route stone** | A stone carrying a Keeper's cut (a naming carved in it) | Stone; it doesn't turn |

### 3.6 Naming and the Keeper's hand [shipped]

A naming says where a lamp stands so a stranger can find their way by it. Not after anyone, not
for anyone. Its form (Wenna's rule, *The Form of a Naming*):

> **"You are the first lamp past the Whitequiet. Behind you the road; ahead the old shrine.
> Hold fast."**

ordinal, relation, landmark; what lies behind; what lies ahead; a closing. The closings are
**Keep the weave tight. / Hold fast. / Stay.** The short name comes from the same parts:
"First lamp past the Whitequiet". A naming is made from parts, never free words; once given it
stays, even if the land around it moves, as history.

**The Keeper's hand** is the craft of naming, taught by Mara after the Lantern Road: copy a road
stone's cut, check it against the ledger, then keep a communal lamp. Anyone may learn it; no
class, no Habitica level. With it you can set and name way-lamps. The first naming of a lamp
leaves **lamp ash**.

**The Keeper** (capital K) is Hearthwick's keeper of the ledger and the road: Hesper, then Wenna,
now Mara. **A Keeper** is anyone with the Keeper's hand.

### 3.7 Land: Wild, Common, Built [shipped]

| Tier | In the world | What keeps it |
|---|---|---|
| **Wild** | Unwalked, unheld land | Nothing. Its detail turns every wick; its earth's shape stays |
| **Common** | Laid paths and communal lamps | One visit to that stretch each wick. Unvisited work turns; its stone and timber go to Pell's |
| **Built** | Lot backland and camps, held by a lamp's oil | Oil at the turning. An empty lamp **dims** for one wick (a warning in the world and on the map), then turns; everything comes back (3.8) |
| **Permanent** | Hearthwick town, each lot and hearth, and the Tangle | The Wick Tree's reach (2.8). *[later]* a chartered Wilds hearth's core plot, by a co-signed promise |

Never promise "for good" for any land in the Wilds.

### 3.8 Pell's crate [shipped]

Pell is the under-clerk the Tarrow Count House sent up to keep the Commons plot ledger. His
crate came down his family: his father gave it to him, and it has passed from Pell to Pell for
generations. Nobody knows whether an ancestor made it or was given it. The Pells have spent
generations trying to understand the land and the way it gives things back; that is how a family
of clerks came to keep deeds.

When a camp's lamp goes dark, a camp is packed up, a path goes unwalked or an unvisited
communal post turns, the drift gives the made things back to the crate, for the person they
belong to: "The woods turned. Pell has your things." Camp plants come back still growing.

A **lot's backland** is the exception: it comes home rather than to Pell's. When a lot lantern
goes dark, the backland's pieces go to the household's home storage and its plants come back as
seeds in the shared chest; only paths laid there go to Pell's crate. The Pell family ledgers also record things that did **not** come back: only the old
world's things, never a player's. Some of them are the trinkets the Whitequiet gives back, so Pell
reads the same mystery as the Six from the other end. A player's own things always come back.

### 3.9 Ways home [shipped]

- **Turncaps** are grey mushrooms whose caps lean toward the nearest held light (a biological
  compass). They are named after the old chimney **cowls** on roofs: a cowl spins with the wind
  all its life, but after twenty winters it settles leaning toward the nearest light that held,
  and the mushrooms lean the same way. "Turncap" means the mushroom; the roof fitting is a cowl. A **jar of turncaps** steadies the unmoored. A **homing turncap** is one set in amber
  while it still leans toward town: hold still a moment and it takes you back to the north arch.
- **Lamp ash** from a first naming, sewn into a **lamp-ash pouch**, remembers its lamp: hold still
  and it takes you to that lamp, if it still burns.

Neither works in a fight. Walking is always free.

### 3.10 Time [shipped]

A wick is seven days; twelve wicks make a year; four **marks** make the seasons. The world
compresses time, and the lore doesn't count the days in a year: the turning world makes them
hard to count. Story years ("thirty years ago") are story years.

**Wicks:** Thaw, Mud, Bud, Bloom, Light, Cart, Haze, Sap, Amber, Leaf, Smoke, Quiet.

**Marks:** **Mudrise** (Thaw–Bud: breakup and freshet), **Carting** (Bloom–Cart: long light, the
safest season; growth is itself a remembering), **Amberfall** (Haze–Amber: the sap tide), **Quiet**
(Leaf–Quiet: cold is stillness, and rest).

## 4. History

### 4.1 Eras

### 4.2 The Compact and the Weir Years [shipped as story]

The **Carters' Compact** swore every carter to three duties on Carting Day: **Light** (never
leave the chain dark; the last cart out lights behind itself), **Log** (log your load at the
Count House; no unlogged amber, no unmarked oak), **Leave** (leave the road as you'd wish to find
it). The golden rule: *never two dark in a row.* The toll was a **wheel-tax**, a copper per wheel
per lit league. The Compact's road watch were **wardens**. Its last generation, the people the
older villagers remember, some called **the Company of the Road**.

In the Weir Years the road was held rigid through all four seasons, because Sallow Ford wanted
more amber and Tarrow more oak. The drift was not cancelled, only displaced, building against
the edges of the light like water behind a weir (Hemlock's **Weir Effect**; his warning was
suppressed). That was ordinary greed and good intentions, and the Dark worked the crack it made.

**Hesper** was the Keeper of the Weir Years, sharp and tireless, and she could not bear to let
the road rest. When Orrin built the great bridge at the Tangle crossing, Hesper named its iron
bracket joint so it would never lean. Dorrit Venn, Orrin's apprentice, argued for oak trunnels
that would let the joint flex ("if the hill wants to wander, do we step with it, or remind it to
stay put?"). Hesper's joint was a binding, not a naming: it ordered the ground instead of
reminding it. Orrin carved it; Hesper bound it.

### 4.3 The Winter of Two Storms [shipped as story]

Keeper **Wenna Hale**, Hesper's successor, kept the road when the pressure broke.

1. **The first storm (Quiet-wick 7th).** A white-out; the road burned through its oil ("Oil, 3
   casks short"). Wenna closed the road: she cut the closure mark, *two weaves and a break*, into
   the route stones and snuffed the chain from the hilltop shrine downward. Dark on purpose, like
   a fence. Closure Night keeps the date.
2. **Hesper walks out.** Hesper, old and long retired, would not accept the
   closure. On the first night of the lull she walked out alone to the Tangle crossing to hold
   it herself. She did not come back. The village believes the white-out took her; only Wenna
   suspected. Orrin learns what his joint did when the player does.
3. **The lull and the Lull Run.** A calm came over the turn of the year, and nine calm days were
   *forecast*. Hollis Brack wanted to run a train to Sallow Ford for oil; Wenna refused ("the
   ninth day is not a promise; it is a guess"). After five calm days, at dawn on the sixth
   (Thaw-wick 6th), **the Six** went *under the mark* anyway, on Maren Brack's wheels, carrying
   storm-grade amber. Silas turned back at the Brackenwood fork. That first day out, Tam crushed
   two fingers in the yoke-chain, and Nan cast them in storm-grade amber.
4. **The second storm (Thaw-wick 7th).** It came on the seventh day of the lull, three days
   before the forecast ran out, and it was a **stir**. Hesper's bound joint
   would not flex; the ground tore around it, and the bridge broke in half, as Dorrit had
   warned. **Bett Cooley** and **Dorrit Venn** fell with the carts. The other four pushed on and
   reached **Sallow Ford**. Behind them the road forgot its shape and the chain went dark. The
   Tangle is the scar of that night. Hesper's binding cost Bett and Dorrit their lives.

The found texts' dates follow this timeline (seven-day wicks: day 1 to 7, never higher).

### 4.5 After [shipped]

- **The Stone Warden.** That spring, searchers wanted to go in. In eleven days Wenna and
  Orrin built the Warden at Ashwatch: an iron-oak frame from Dorrit's ruined span, a coat of
  drift-stone, a heart-lamp of storm-grade amber. They kept watch a whole night so the stone
  learned its pose. Wenna gave it one naming, cut beside the closure mark on the Ashwatch route
  stone: *"The road is closed here. Behind you the village, ahead the dark. Hold the break."*
  Because drift-stone moves when unwatched, the Warden walks, but only ever back to its pose.
  Turn the naming round, *the road is held again; rest*, and speak it to the heart-lamp, and it
  settles. It stopped Silas from following Hollis that second spring. Blows only clink off it;
  *[later]* attacking it makes things worse. **The Warden is the honest opposite of a binding:**
  it was named, not ordered, and its naming can be turned round.
- **Wenna's secret.** By the third autumn a carved pine fox with a long **right** ear washed down
  the Wend into the mill grate. Silas carves his foxes mirror-wise, long ear on the left; Hollis
  carved them on the right. Aldo Tolley the miller brought it to Wenna, and she knew four had
  lived. She also knew the village would march into the dark Wilds and die if she told them.
  Every autumn another fox came down; Aldo kept them in a linseed box, and Wenna added a tally
  to the last page of her ledger, in Keeper's script. Wenna's silence was the hardest care, not
  hoarding.
- **The account is still open.** The Six never settled their tally at the Count House, so the
  Compact never struck them off the roll ("Account 404 stays open"). Wenna's last page is not a
  memorial; it is a ledger of shares and a standing order: *keep the account open.*

### 4.6 What waits [later]

- **Dorrit's Span** (0.15). Hesper is still at the Tangle crossing, bound into Orrin's iron
  brackets: one of the obsessed. The span itself fights; Hesper can be redeemed by unwinding her
  binding the way the Warden is settled. The rebuilt span is Dorrit's: it steps with the hill.
  The stakes are Orrin's (he carved the joint), Ada's (Bett fell there) and Mara's (Hesper
  taught Wenna).
- **The far light.** When the road's far lamps are relit, a second point of gold blinks back from
  the horizon: the Sallow Ford lamp. The account is still open, and the last cart is coming home.

## 5. Workings and the Practices [later]

### 5.1 Workings

You don't cast spells; you perform **workings**: earned light worked into stone, spark, voice,
hearth or root. A whole working rests on three things together:

- **cadence**: the breath, rhythm or intent that wakes it (a naming is a cadence);
- **vessel**: the mark, tool or form that shapes and holds it (a lamp, a rune, a cut);
- **care**: the honest effort that feeds it (oil, glims).

The anchor-light (3.4) is the working everyone knows. *[shipped]* Fingersnap, the starter's bolt,
is a working too.

### 5.2 The five Practices

| Practice | Refined by | Quiet craft | Sharp edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Handcraft** (runes and stone) | Shaping clay, wood and iron; maker's marks | Runes that hold land, reinforce bridges; masterwork tools and kits | Rune-anchors that block charges and hold safe ground |
| **Waycraft** (sparks and signs) | Braving the unknown, clearing trails, fighting | Reads the Turning, lights way-beacons, senses where the Dark pools | Reflex workings: Fingersnap, interrupts, dashes |
| **Songcraft** (cadence and names) | Restoring poems and ballads, music, keeping history | Pieces lost papers into songs, wakes shrines, names lamps with lasting power | Countersongs that strip a boss's frenzy and steady allies |
| **Hearthcraft** (ember and bond) | Tending lamps, cooking, hosting, helping friends | Co-signs hearths, refines oils, makes provisions | Sanctuary: shared damage, revives, burning back shadow |
| **Wildcraft** (root, water and beast) | Fishing, pets, gardens and reeds, weather | Companions find caches; rare reeds and clay; reads deep water | Beasts that harry foes; roots, mist and water as snares |

The Practices replace the four class-locked crafts in `design/magic.md`. A player's Habitica
class is a **lean**, a head start in one Practice: Warrior, Handcraft; Mage, Songcraft; Healer,
Hearthcraft; Rogue, Waycraft. Practice ranks, generalists and specialists, and cross-workings
(from the owner's magic bible §4) are the starting point of a later systems design. Anyone may
learn the Keeper's hand regardless (3.6).

### 5.3 Overreach

Every honest working takes patience, presence or shared effort. Forcing one past honest care
**cracks** it, and the Dark slips into the crack, drawing warmth out of the land around it and
slowly hollowing out the worker. The forms:

- **dead-binding**: a coercive rune so you never have to tend a thing again (Fallbrine's way,
  and Hesper's joint);
- **scorching**: burning glims greedily for raw destruction;
- **compulsion**: using a naming to puppet a living thing;
- **hoarding**: locking a hearth inward out of fear;
- **obsession**: refusing to let a working rest.

The temptation to take any of these is called **the Short Way**, and those who take it are
**short-wayers**. ("The Old Ways" is kept for the paired-lamp travel working, *[later]*.)

### 5.4 The fallen, the beasts and the things of the Dark

*Settled with the owner, 2026-10-20 (`docs/planning/wide-world/responses/creature-lore-argon.md`).*

Everything a traveller meets in the Wilds stands in its own relation to the Turning and the Dark:

- **The fallen.** Fallen people have no single name. Villagers say someone is **lost**; Keepers
  write that they are **obsessed** or **bound**; children have their own words. In the game a
  fallen person goes by their own name ("Hesper, at the span"), never a category. They keep their
  cunning, craft and workings, now turned to hoarding light and hunting anyone who carries a
  lantern.
- **Ordinary animals** [later]. Squirrels and hares in the woods, ducks and kingfishers by the
  lakes, mountain goats on the Tops, glow-moths and blind fish in the caves. Like the trees and
  streams, they belong to the hurting earth that still tries to remember itself every Turning.
  They are harmless and go about their own small work: a goat climbs a real trail up a crag; a
  kingfisher darts toward clay or a shallow spit; a cave-moth drifts toward daylight. Watching
  them helps a traveller read the land.
- **Unmoored beasts** [shipped as beetles; the rest later]. Beetles, boars, mire-toads,
  marsh-crabs, rams and cave-crickets. Animals have no pride or greed for the Dark to whisper to,
  so where the land lies far from tended light and the Dark's pressure leans in, it works on
  their fear, hunger and confusion at the Turning. A smoky **fever-crust** gathers on them and
  turns them against lamplight. Fought down to the point of breaking, a beast staggers, and the
  player chooses how the fight ends:
  - **Break the spell.** Answer the fever with a **glim**, a raised light or a **working**
    (Fingersnap, or a Practice's calming craft). The fever-crust shatters off as shed material,
    the animal's eyes clear, and it flees into the brush, or stays in that place as an ordinary
    animal for the rest of the wick.
  - **Defeat by force.** Strike through the stagger and slay the beast outright, for its intact
    parts and trophies.
- **Things of the Dark** [shipped as wisps; the rest later]. Wisps in the woods,
  **fen-lanterns** in the marshes (they mimic the warm gold of a communal lamp from afar, then
  turn cold violet up close), **draught-motes** on windy passes, and **shadow-leeches** along the
  rim of a lantern's light in caves. They were never animals and never alive: they are knots of
  cold from the Crack, formed round a mockery of light, wind or shadow. Nothing living is trapped
  inside them; beaten, they disperse into cold air and ash.
- **Creatures of the Whitequiet** [later]. In the Whitequiet the earth has stopped trying to
  remember itself (2.4), so there is no struggle for the Dark to fever and no warmth for things
  of the Dark to drink. The Whitequiet has no fights. Its **white moths** and **pale harts** are
  hushed impressions of woodland life: moths spiral above buried trinkets, and a pale hart will
  lead a slow, patient walker toward a route stone or back to living land.
- **Holdouts** [later]. A few survivors of the Company still hold out alone in remote corners of
  the Wilds, wary, waiting to see whether the new Hearthwick keeps its word. Drop-in outposts.

### 5.5 Choices with the fallen

How a group meets one of the fallen leaves a lasting mark on their world:

- **Break the binding by force.** Win the fight; take the fallen one's gear and trophies. The
  binding is shattered, not unwound, and the Dark bleeds down into the ground beneath: a
  repeatable, dangerous delve opens there.
- **Unwind it.** Find their letters, mend their lamp, piece together their song from the library,
  shield the singer in the last phase. The hold breaks without killing them; they remember their
  name and become a keeper of that place, teaching what they know.
- **Together.** Fighters break the armour so makers and singers can finish the redemption.

Failure costs time and a repair fee, never items.

## 6. Places

### 6.1 Hearthwick [shipped]

One fixed town around the Wick Tree, with roads into the Wilds on every side.

- **The Square**: the Wick Tree, the hearth-stone and its lantern, the town's one well and one
  notice board.
- **The Commons**: the old staging ground of the Lantern Road, now part of town. Players always
  see "the Commons". Old carters call it **the Carters' Apron**; the name lives only in their
  speech and in old texts. Every Carting Day a polished hame hangs on its gate for the cart that
  never came home. Elara camps there.
- **The north arch** (the Tangle Arch): the way north into the Tangle. Homing turncaps lead here.
- **The East Gate**: the Lantern Road's way out, to Brackenwood, Ashwatch and the shrine.
- **The West Road** to the Tops and Tarrow; **the Low Road**, by which the player arrives.
- Hazel's bakery, Finn's mill on the Wend, Ada's house with its window lamp, the library (the
  Keepers' reading room), the chandler, Pell's deeds office, Silas's yard, the Wheel & Wick
  guildhouse (being reopened), the lots and their backland.

### 6.2 Along the old road [shipped]

The Lantern Road left by the East Gate, climbed through **Brackenwood** (iron-oak and bracken) to
**Ashwatch** (the Keeper's old waystation; the route stone with the closure mark; the Stone
Warden on the path) and the **hilltop shrine** and its lantern. From the shrine it swung north
through **the Tangle**, over **the Tangle crossing** (Orrin's bridge; **Dorrit's Span**), and on
through **the Whitequiet** toward **Sallow Ford**, north-east. Its lamps are dark lamp stones
now, and route stones lean in the moss.

### 6.3 The wider world [story]

- **Sallow Ford**: north-east, where oil was pressed. The four survivors reached it; Hollis, Tam
  and Joss live there now (Nan died there). The Sallow
  Ford Institute of Cartography; the ferry ruins.
- **Tarrow-on-the-Reed**: west over the Tops; the river market. The **Count House** (tolls,
  tallies and Pell's employer) and the **Oak Hall** (iron-oak charters; the oak-mark, a wheel and
  a wave).
- **Sennick's Ferry**: a chain-ferry on the Reed. In the old days of the running drift the Sennicks
  cut the chain and moved the ferry whenever the river wandered (2.1).
- **The Tops and the Oaker Hills**: charcoal burners and smallholdings over the ridge.
- **The Sapping Grounds** and **the Amberwash**: amber country; pale gravel scars.
- **The Merrow Saltings**: fen country far south-west, where people ride the drift like a tide.
- **Lake country** *[later, 0.16]*: shores, clay, reeds, shallow caves.

## 8. The Keeper's voice

Mara's voice is careful, dry affection: brisk, practical, warm underneath, in threes. She writes
in pencil, because "ink is for what's done." The Keeper's notices are ledger-plain:

- "Dark of Amber-wick — the outer Wilds will turn."
- "The woods turned. Pell has your things."
- "The drift turned while you stood in it."

Wenna's voice survives in the ledger: terse entries, *Noted*, and the cut. Never write the Keeper
as mystical; a naming is bookkeeping said aloud.

## 10. Tone

- Memory and bookkeeping over mysticism. A naming is telling the truth about a place.
- The Wild is hurt, not evil; the Dark is evil. Never make darkness itself the enemy: lit means
  remembered, dark means forgotten. The enemy is the thing that wants it to stay forgotten.
- Danger is mild and defeat a stumble; nothing a player made is ever lost.
- Tactile: woodsmoke, the weight of amber, the mill wheel.
- No chosen ones and no destinies. The player matters because they show up and keep promises.
- Never name Habitica or real-life tasks in the world; say glims and kept promises.
- Rest is part of care. Don't glorify grinding.
