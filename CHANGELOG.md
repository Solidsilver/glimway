# Changelog

What changed in each release of Glimway. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) (0.x for now: anything may still
change). How to cut a release: [docs/releasing.md](docs/releasing.md).

Each release has two parts, always in this order and with these headings, so
the game can show the first part as "What's new":

- `### For players`: short, plain lines about what you'll notice in play.
- `### Technical`: what changed for people who run or work on Glimway.

## [Unreleased]

### For players

- Glimway's home-screen icon and favicon now show the glowing Wick Tree.
- The world map shows held land, common paths and dimming warnings. Open Details for the Lamps ledger: see each known light’s reserve and what happens at the turning, then pick a row to find it on the map.
- Signing in, reloading and stepping outdoors no longer freeze the game for a moment as the world appears.
- Choose who may build at your camp. Look at your way-lamp and pick "Who may build here": just your household, your household and people you name, everyone but people you name, or everyone in the world. Anyone can still walk through your camp, pick forage there and add oil, and you can always pick up your own things. Felling trees and breaking stone at a camp count as building. You can also move someone else's piece off your camp; it waits for them in a crate at Pell's office.
- Once a wick, tap the Wick Tree in Hearthwick for a jar of its sap. The chandler refines it into hearth oil for free: enough to keep a lot lantern burning a wick. You don't need a lantern to tap. A missed wick's sap doesn't wait for you, and more oil is still for sale at the chandler.
- The Wilds keep their shape across the turning: the Wend runs the same course and woods and meadows stay where they are. Only the living detail (trees, forage, camps, chests, places and paths) changes each wick.
- The Wilds turn early once, when this version arrives: the current wick's land is replaced. The Tangle keeps everything.
- Light your lot lantern: fill the lantern on your lot's back line with hearth oil and the woods behind your lot hold still through the turning. While it burns, that land is part of your yard: set pieces out there and plant in it. Each flask burns for a wick, up to three; anyone may top up a neighbour's lantern. Run dry, it gutters for a wick, then goes dark, and the woods turn; anything you set out there comes home to your storage, and planted seeds come back to your chest.
- The chandler by the square sells hearth oil, so you can keep your light without amber.
- Set out a way-lamp in the Wilds to make a camp. Craft one at your bench (six timber, four stone, a lamp head and a lamp wick), then set it down from your pack with a flask of hearth oil, and the land it lights holds through the turning. Set your home pieces out and plant there; friends can top up your lamp. When the oil runs out it gutters for a wick, then the camp goes, and everything you had there waits for you in a crate at Pell's office. Nothing is lost.
- With reduced motion on, an enemy's "!" warning no longer pops, a defeated enemy fades without sinking, and your hero no longer squashes when you're knocked out.
- Paths you lay in the Wilds are common ways now: if anyone walks through that bit of land during the wick, the path is still there after the turning, even though the trees around it change. Land nobody visits turns, and its stone or timber waits for whoever laid it in a crate at Pell's office. Paths behind a lot lantern that goes dark go to Pell's the same way.
- Way-lamps now have their own stone-footed, hooded lamp art in the Wilds and a matching pack icon.
- Relight the old lamp stones along the Lantern Road. Look at a dark stone and light it with a lamp wick; no oil, and anyone can do it. While someone passes that way each wick, it stays lit through the turning. Left alone for a wick, it goes dark again. Lit lamps show on the map as common lamps, with whether anyone has been by this wick.
- Set a way-lamp as a communal lamp instead of a camp: choose "Set it as a communal lamp" in your pack. It takes no oil and holds no land, and anyone passing keeps it lit. Only you can take it down. If nobody comes by for a whole wick, it waits for you in a crate at Pell's office.
- After the Lantern Road, Mara has one more favour: copy the cut from the first lamp stone north of the Whitequiet and bring it to her ledger. She makes you a Keeper's hand, with a lamp head, a lamp wick, *The Form of a Naming* and the recipe pages for the homing turncap and the lamp-ash pouch. Then light or name a common lamp, or tend or read one someone else lit; any of those finishes it.
- Keepers name lamps from parts, never typed words: which lamp it is, where it stands, a landmark nearby, what's behind and ahead, and a closing ("Hold fast."). The naming shows in the world and in the map's Lamps ledger with who named it. A lamp is named once, and gives a pinch of lamp ash the first time.
- Setting a way-lamp for a camp now takes a Keeper's hand. Camps already set stay as they are, and anyone can still set a communal lamp.
- Two ways home. A homing turncap (one turncap spawn and two amber) takes you back to Hearthwick's north arch. A lamp-ash pouch (one lamp ash and two fiber) takes you to a way-lamp or common lamp you bound it to: stand by the lamp and choose "Bind it to the lamp here". Make them at your bench once you have their recipe pages; Mara gives you both when she makes you a Keeper's hand. Either one needs you to hold still for three seconds, and neither works in a fight. It's only used up when it takes you there: if the lamp has gone dark, been packed away at Pell's or burns in another world, you keep it and it tells you why.
- A lamp keeps its name wherever you set it. Name a communal lamp or a camp's way-lamp once and the name goes with it, even to another world, and its lamp ash comes only that once. Lantern Road stones are named in each world.
- Set a way-lamp from your pack without a homestead. Camps, communal lamps and camp pieces no longer need a Hearthwick deed.
- Craft a trowel at your bench (one timber, one stone) to dig up what you've planted. Hold it and use it on a plant in your yard, your lit backland or a camp, and the plant comes up whole into your pack, ready to plant again and still growing from where it was. Each dig wears it, like any tool. At a camp, anyone can look at a plant, but only those who may build there, and whoever planted it, can dig it up. Plants no longer offer "Dig it up" when you look at them.
- Keepers can pack up a whole camp at once. Look at your way-lamp and choose "Pack up the camp". Everything waits at Pell's, each thing for whoever it belongs to, and Pell tells them you moved it.
- Paths you laid in the Wilds before this version aren't lost with the old land: the stone and timber you paid for them waits for you in a crate at Pell's office.
- If a home's deed is lost, nothing on the lot is lost with it. Everything there (pieces in the house, yard and backland, the shared chest and gate shelf, plants, and unburnt lantern oil) waits in a crate at Pell's office for the last member to leave, and timber on the woodpile goes to whoever stacked it.
- A camp you take down and set again somewhere else in the same wick keeps its new place at the turning. When nobody comes by for several turnings, each one is still counted, so lamps burn and dim the same as if people had been around.
- Everyone at a camp now sees whose each plant and piece is, not only its keeper. When you pack up your own camp, Pell greets you about your own things instead of saying the woods turned.
- The world map no longer misses a lamp that stands alone on common land.
- Choose what to take from Pell's crate. Ask Pell to "Choose what to take" and pick things one at a time, or take everything at once. Whatever you leave stays with Pell for as long as you like.
- A plant in Pell's crate comes back as the plant itself, not just its seed. Plant it again in your yard, your lit backland or a camp you build at, and it carries on growing from where it was. You can still take it as its seed if you'd rather.
- A relit lamp stone on the Lantern Road now shows its wick burning in the niche, and the stone with Wenna's cut beside it (the Keeper's-hand stone) has its carved panel, dark or lit.
- With the map's lamps shown, each lamp has a mark where it stands, paler while it's dimming.
- Camps push back the edge of the Wilds. Land within three chunks of any camp or lit backland is open to walk, gather and build in, so a camp set near the edge, and another set further out, lets you push deeper into the Wilds together. When a camp goes dark at the turning, its edge land goes back to the drift. Anyone left standing out there finds themselves on the nearest open ground still in reach, carrying everything they had.
- A small chip at the top of the screen says what the land under your feet is in the Wilds: held (your camp, a friend's camp, your lot or someone's lot), common (kept by people walking through) or wild (its trees and finds change at the next turning). Land whose lamp is gone says "Turning": it turns at the next one. When the lamp holding the land is down to its last wick of oil, the chip turns amber and says it's dimming, a wick before the lamp starts guttering. Tap or hover over it for a sentence about what that means.
- When your camp's way-lamp or your lot's lantern is down to its last wick, the game tells you once, the next time you play. You won't hear it if you're standing right by the lamp. Your lot lantern in each world you play gets its own notice.
- A lamp on its last wick of oil now looks dimmed straight away, in the world and on the map, and says so when you look at it. Before, it looked lit until it started guttering a wick later.
- The land chip changes as soon as you light, fill, tend or take down a lamp, or lay or lift a path, and if it can't read the land it tries again by itself. After you move to another world, you no longer see the old world's camps for a moment.
- The map calls ordinary wild land "Wild · changes each Turning".
- The hearth-oil note mentions the Wick Tree's weekly sap as well as the chandler.
- A recipe you haven't learned says "not learned yet" instead of "page not found".
- If you walk away while a lamp action is still on its way, you still see how it went, but the lamp-naming panel no longer opens behind you.
- While you name a lamp, J, C and I no longer open panels behind the naming screen.
- Pell's crate keeps its buttons off until the list catches up after a Take, so a quick second press no longer says "Nothing waiting".
- The gift shelf no longer stalls. What you take, buy or stock there no longer flicks back to an older view, and a refusal is shown once instead of being asked again.
- Step into a camp and open your pack, and it offers that camp's buttons straight away.
- A turncap, a lamp-ash pouch or any other move that arrives during a Turning now takes you there once the veil lifts.
- After travelling home by turncap or lamp-ash pouch, a reload or a dropped connection no longer puts you back where you set out. If the trip only goes through later, the game tells you. Being moved back inside the world's edge now sticks too.
- A reload no longer starts you a few steps back from where you were.
- A camp refused for want of oil, or because the ground is blocked, no longer pushes the edge of the Wilds out for a moment.
- The trowel and the map no longer treat backland or camp land as lit when its lantern ran out before the game caught up.

### Technical

- Migrations 038–048 are folded into one, `038_lamps.sql`, with the same final schema; 001–037 are unchanged. The migration numbers 039–048 in the lines below now name parts of it, and `migration_047_backfill.go` is `migration_038_backfill.go`. A database that ran the old 038–048 (`v0.12.0-alpha.1`, glim-dev) refuses to start with an out-of-order migration error: reset it (docs/home-server.md). New `glimway-server migrate --dry-run COPY` rehearses an upgrade: it runs the `verify-backup` checks on a copy, migrates a private snapshot of it, checks again, and prints the migrations applied and the counts of accounts, worlds, homes, items, plants, holds, camps and Pell's crates before and after. It also sums item stacks, plants and crate entries by item before and after, reconciles 038's stone and timber refunds per account against the paid path tiles in the copy (counting the ground rows the backfill skips: malformed, no author, a gone account), and exits non-zero on anything that doesn't add up. It never writes `COPY`, and refuses `-db` or a file that looks in use. The release flow now rehearses on a copy of the live backup (docs/releasing.md).
- Server settings file (docs/design/server-settings.md): TOML at `-config` / `GLIMWAY_CONFIG` with `[dev]`, `[server.*]` and `[web.*]` tables, layered defaults → file → `GLIMWAY_<PATH>` variables → flags. Unknown keys are refused, and SIGHUP reloads the live settings. Every setting, its default and live-or-restart: `deploy/glimway.example.toml` (generated; `go test ./server/internal/settings -run TestExample -update`). `check-config` checks a file. `GET /api/web-settings` hands the client its `[web]` part per account. `server.login.*` is the first existing knob in the file (the `-login-*` flags and `GLIMWAY_LOGIN_*` still win). Dev mode for a staging server: `[dev] enable`/`accounts` with per-tool switches. A deployed dev build gates `/api/dev/*` on the signed-in account, never the loopback address (any proxied request now counts as remote), and a dev build with a settings file always has a movable clock. The dev panel gains a clock control behind a confirm. The web's dev code follows `__GLIMWAY_DEV_TOOLS__` (Vite dev or `GLIMWAY_DEV_TOOLS=1`). NixOS: `services.<name>.settings` (rendered to `/etc/<name>/config.toml`, checked at build, `reloadTriggers` + `ExecReload`). `settings.dev.enable` picks the dev packages (`glimway-server-dev`, `glimway-web-dev`), and only `glimway-dev` (`allowDevMode`) may set it. A release build (without `-tags dev`) refuses to start with `dev.enable`. Docker: `--build-arg GLIMWAY_DEV_TOOLS=1`, and compose passes `GLIMWAY_CONFIG`. The live NixOS unit gains the settings file and `ExecReload`, and its login flags move into `[server.login]` with the same values, so the deploy of this version restarts it once (docs/releasing.md).
- Lamp art: relit road stones draw the open-map pass's `lamp-stone-relit-*` loop over the dark stone, and the Keeper's-hand quest stone (`cut_stone`) draws `route-stone-dark`/`route-stone-relit-*` (`roadStoneArt` in `src/lib/communal-lamps.ts`). `__fsCommunalLamps()` also returns the stones it drew; new spec `e2e/lamp-art.spec.ts`. The map's Lamps layer marks each lamp with its own icon, `map-lamp-lit` or `map-lamp-dimming` (`MAP_LAMP_ICON` and `mapLampMark` in `src/lib/wilds/map-lamps.ts`). `way-lamp-0` stays unused: no game state shows a dark way-lamp.
- Frontier reach from held land (lamps.md §2.2): `frontier.Inside(world, …)`/`Rect(world)` add every chunk within content `frontier.heldSlack` (3) of a row in `holds`, cached per world in `package frontier` and kept in step by `store/reach.go`: `Hold` and `judgeHolds` stage the world's keys in their transaction, which alone reads them; the store's SQLite driver publishes the staged reach only once the commit succeeds, and drops it on a rollback or a failed commit (a rollback to a savepoint stages again, `RefreshReach`). Presence and fight checks read the published reach. `store.Open` loads all. Callers that passed `""` now pass the world (`wildsWhereInside`, presence room joins, `story.EntityChunk`, ways-home landing). `WildsFrontier.held` names the keys in the near read; the client's `insideFrontier`/`frontierRect` take them (`setHeldReach`), and the scene's bounds follow (`followFrontier`). `maintainState` runs `settleReach`: a saved Wilds place outside reach moves (new place version) to the nearest open tile in reach, else the turncap's anchor. No migration.
- Land chip and dimming notices (lamps.md §1.1, §2.5; world-map.md §13). `ChunkLandStatus` gains `keeper` (a camp setter's display name) and `yours` (the reader set the camp or lives on the lot), filled in by `chunkTier`, which now takes the reader's account. The client's chip is `src/lib/wilds/land-chip.ts` (words in `src/content/land-chip.ts`). `src/game/wilds/land-here.ts` runs it from the window's update and only does work when the chunk or the store version changes. It emits `EV.landHere` into `ui.landHere`, and `Hud.svelte` draws it as a status chip. On entering a chunk, `refreshLand` reads that one chunk's status from `POST /api/wilds/map`, because the near read's occupied chunk can be a step behind; it reuses an answer for 10 s per chunk and wick, keeps `landSeq` ordering, and reports whether an answer came. A failed read is asked again while you stand there, backing off. Any of your operations that changes land (a lamp set, filled, tended or taken down; a path laid or lifted) calls `invalidateLand`, which bumps the store's `landRevision`: the chunk underfoot is read again (once a read still out for it has come back), and others as you step in. A world move or the turning starts the asking over. New `GET /api/lamp-notices` (`api/lamp_notices.go`, `LampNoticesRead` in camps.proto) returns the caller's lit camps and household lot lanterns that are out of oil (last wick or guttering) and haven't been told this episode. Each one is recorded as an `outcomes` row `lamp-dimming:<kind>:<id>:<episode start>` (this wick for a last wick, the wick before for a guttering lamp), and one the caller stands by is recorded without being returned. `Village.tellLampNotices` asks with every calendar read and shows a toast. No migration and no new error codes. New spec `e2e/land-chip.spec.ts`, mapped in `e2e/changed-map.json`.
- Fix: a Turning that started in the first moments of a scene build (signing in or warping as the wick turned) left the world dark under a live HUD. The scene's dark start lifted its hold but skipped the fade-in while `transitioning` was set, and the Turning's hold sets it too. The hold is now `FadeHold` (`src/game/scenes/fade-hold.ts`, tested in `tests/fade-hold.test.ts`): only a newer build or a move away (`moveTo`) takes its fade-in. `e2e/rumour.spec.ts` waits for a turning that `wickSlack` caused to finish before it warps, because a warp during the Turning's hold is dropped.

- Docs: lamps.md records the owner's decision (2026-10-17) that the lamp itself holds the oil (three wicks), with no separate linked store, and notes that `wick-sap` has its icon (`item-wick-sap`). world-map.md §6 no longer describes lamps as a future 0.9 plan.
- CI: pushes to `exp/*` lane branches run only the E2E specs their changes touch (`test:changed` against `origin/dev`) on the self-hosted GPU runner; `dev` and `main` keep the full suite, and so does a manual run with the new `full` input (`gh workflow run ci.yml --ref <branch> -f full=true`). Steadier specs: positions, the Turning and server answers are waited on rather than timed, and the slowest specs are trimmed (docs/testing.md).

The gift shelf shows its first good read instead of waiting for the newest.

  residents on an hourly cycle (`content/residents.json`, `residentAt`,
  the server's clock via `X-Glimway-Now`), quests by id on the server
  (triggers, gates in order, `reached_at`/`gate_at`, migration 029).
- Furnishings: one catalogue (`content/furnishings.json`, 76 pieces,
  the home goods moved in) and one placement rule (`canPlace`, Go and
  TS, shared vectors); rooms place pieces by id with a facing and a
  parent; collision is each piece's base.
- The indoors art pass, round 2 (176 frames) and a shared interior kit,
  under design 7.0's interior style rules.
- Reports: closing the tab sends the newest steps under the next
  sequence instead of resending the report in flight.
- Dev mode for local playtesting (dev builds only, which `npm run server`
  now makes): `POST /api/dev/grant` gives the signed-in account embers,
  items or home goods through the real store paths, never anything from
  Habitica; the dev panel (`` ` `` or the Menu's Dev row) exists only under
  `vite` dev. Production binaries and bundles don't contain either.
- e2e waits for a server answer allow 15 s (`SERVER_ANSWER_MS`): the first
  answers of a session take 3-5 s on the software-rendered smoke runner.
- Everything drawn for a tile registers in one place
  (`src/game/area/tile-art.ts`), so felling removes the foreground canopy
  too; a dev-only `__fsArtAt(tx, ty)` hook lets e2e read what's drawn.
- Dialogue busts for Mara, Orrin and Pip on a new sheet
  (`fingersnap-portraits-residents`); every speaker maps to a bust, and the
  sprite crop is only the fallback.
- Server-first (`docs/design/server-first.md`): the server owns all state
  and rules. `PUT /api/progress` and the uploaded progress document are
  gone; the client sends operations (`proto/glimway/v1/op.proto`,
  `operations.proto`), predicts their answers and rolls back a refusal.
  Every operation is idempotent on its key, replays the current state, and
  runs in a savepoint. Contract 3, sent as `X-Glimway-Contract`; an older
  client gets a reload notice.
- Accounts: random account ids, looked up from Habitica sign-ins
  (`sign_ins`). Migrations 026 (accounts), 027 (normalized state,
  `wilds_chunks`, the Wilds reset) and 028 (moves the old progress
  documents into the new tables).
- The Wilds generator is in Go (integer-only, v2) and pre-generates nine
  chunks per epoch; the client generator is gone and reads served chunks.
- The client keeps an outbox in IndexedDB per account and device, behind a
  Web Lock, so offline play queues operations and replays them with the
  same keys. Reports carry generations and sequence numbers; places and
  vitals have their own version watermarks.
- Local (guest) play is removed, and with it the client-side progress
  save.
- Dev builds only (`-tags dev`, and only with `-dev-clock`):
  `POST /api/dev/clock` moves a test server's clock forward, so e2e can
  cross a season end. Production binaries don't contain the route.
- CI: the full suite uploads screenshots and error context only on
  failure; `go test -tags dev` covers the dev clock.
- Sound effects from Kenney's CC0 packs (`public/assets/audio/kenney/`,
  43 MP3s, about 121 KB, in Git LFS; register in `ASSETS.md`). One sound
  module (`src/game/sound.ts`) plays them from the event bus; new bus events
  `sound:cue`, `sound:footstep` and `sound:work`. It stays silent under
  automated browsers (`navigator.webdriver`), so e2e runs fetch no audio.
  The setting moved to `glimway:sound` (`fingersnap:muted` is no longer read).
- The canvas renders at the device pixel ratio, capped at 3 (Phaser's NONE
  scale mode, sized by `src/game/main.ts`); the camera zooms in canvas px
  (`canvasZoomFor`) and everything the interface reads or writes (insets,
  the hero's spot, the dev hooks) stays in CSS px through `canvasRatio`.
  Phones above a ratio of 1 keep the whole 4× art: about 44 MB of textures
  instead of 16 MB.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-08

### For players

- When a new version of Glimway is ready, a small notice offers to reload.
  Your progress is saved first, and if it can't be saved just then, the
  notice says so and waits.
- The Menu shows which version you're playing, with a link to this list of
  what's new.
- After an update, a short card says what's new since you last played. The
  Menu's "What's new" brings it back any time.
- Prompts are steadier: the nearest thing you can reach always shows its
  prompt, and a click reaches exactly as far as the action itself.

### Technical

- `package.json` is the one source of the version; the Nix packages read it
  too. Each build also gets a build id: the short git commit of a clean
  checkout, the `GLIMWAY_BUILD` it is given (the Docker build argument the
  release workflow fills, the flake's revision), or else a hash of the build's
  inputs (`scripts/build-version.mjs`).
- The client gets both at build time (`__GLIMWAY_VERSION__`,
  `__GLIMWAY_BUILD__`), and `vite build` writes `dist/version.json`. Open tabs
  check it when they come back into view and every ten minutes; a different
  build id brings up the reload notice. Its Reload stops the world (input,
  enemies, physics, timers), saves until the stored copy is the live game,
  waits for every server write in flight (including ones queued behind the
  upload, and a superseded tab's orphan copy), and only then reloads; if
  anything can't be saved, play resumes and the notice says why.
- The server is built with its version and build id (`-ldflags -X`, `dev`
  otherwise), reports them in `GET /api/health` and logs them at start-up.
- `version.json` is served with `Cache-Control: no-store`.
- The release workflow stops early when the tag doesn't match
  `package.json`'s version.
- This changelog, and the release steps in `docs/releasing.md`.
- The "What's new" card reads each release's `### For players` lines from
  this file when the game is built (`virtual:whats-new`,
  `scripts/whats-new.mjs`), never at runtime. A device remembers the build it
  last caught up to (`glimway:whats-new` in localStorage); a device that
  never saw the card catches up without one. `CHANGELOG.md` is now a build
  input (the build hash, the Docker context, the Nix web package).
- Interface cleanup: one panel shell (`Panel.svelte`) and notice card
  (`NoticeCard.svelte`), shared panel styles and colour tokens in `app.css`,
  the panel action and bus helpers (`panel-state.svelte.ts`), one table of
  what each overlay holds back (`layers.ts`), the camera insets measured
  from marked elements (`play-insets.ts`), connected play's state machine
  out of `App.svelte` (`account-flow.svelte.ts`, unit-tested), and the
  refusal copy in `src/content/errors.ts`. Node tests can import rune
  modules through `tests/helpers/svelte-runes.ts`.
- The client no longer falls back when a server has no mail recall: every
  server since the first deploy has it.
- A shared contract in protobuf (`proto/`, generated with `buf` into
  `server/internal/gen` and `src/lib/gen`, checked in): the error codes are
  one enum on both sides, calendar and invites are served from generated
  types with today's JSON shape, and CI regenerates the code and runs
  `buf breaking` against `main`. Plan for the remaining domains:
  `docs/proto-migration.md`.
- Presence is binary protobuf over the `glimway.presence.v1` subprotocol;
  a broadcast is encoded once for every recipient (about 7.5 times faster).
  A tab from an older build is told to reload (close code 4005).
- Game cleanup: one typed, Phaser-free event bus; `lib/tile.ts` and one
  hash module; one interactions path (each point carries its verb, label,
  marker and reach) with one nearest-wins loop; `WorldScene` split into
  controllers (unmoored, the Turning, dialogue actions, moves); homesteads
  and enemies split by concern; the superseded placeholder drawers retired
  and the art-pass loaders collapsed into `art-pass.ts`.
- Server cleanup: `api.go`, `items.go`, `homestead.go` and `presence.go`
  split by responsibility (`expansion.go` is now `mutation.go`), shared
  HTTP test helpers, `internal/itemmove` for item transfers (API and mail),
  and a pinned migration history (`store/history.json`) with upgrade
  fixtures that carry data.
- Playwright uses at most 3 local workers by default.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-07

The first public release.

### For players

- Walk the old lantern road: meet Mara in Hearthwick, follow the Brackenwood
  Path, settle the stone warden at Ashwatch Ruin, and light the lanterns
  again.
- Light, fair combat: every attack is telegraphed, and you have a basic
  attack, a signature ability for your class and a dodge roll.
- Play as a guest, with everything saved in your browser, or as your own
  Habitica hero: every 10 XP you earn on Habitica becomes an ember to spend
  on warm rests, road lanterns and the Ember Charm.
- Join a small, invite-only world with friends: raise a homestead in
  Hearthwick Commons from a campsite to a cottage and a workshop, arrange
  your furniture, gather in the shared Tangle, give to village projects, send
  mail, and see each other walk around and wave.
- Gather and craft: chop, quarry, dig and plant, then make things at the
  hearth, the desk and the crafting bench.
- Glimway keeps its own calendar, with festivals that change the village for
  a day.
- Find 52 papers (letters, ledgers, songs and more), read them in your
  journal, and share them in the Hearthwick Library.
- Plays on phones too, with touch controls.

### Technical

- Browser game in Svelte 5, TypeScript, Phaser 3 and Vite; an optional Go
  server with SQLite for shared worlds, with presence over a WebSocket.
- Habitica access is read-only: one `GET /user` per connect or sync, from
  the browser.
- Three ways to deploy: a NixOS service flake, Docker Compose with images on
  GHCR (`ghcr.io/solidsilver/glimway`, amd64 and arm64), or by hand
  ([docs/home-server.md](docs/home-server.md)).
- Code under AGPL-3.0-or-later, the game's own art under CC0, Habitica's art
  under its own licence; contributions under the DCO.
- Unit tests, Go tests and Playwright playtests.
